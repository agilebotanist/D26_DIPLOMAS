# AI and the global challenges (UN Sustainable Development Goals)

Added 2026-11-18 at the speaker's request: step back from "our comfortable position is threatened" and ask what AI does for the global goals; the speaker's hypothesis was that the measured impact may be none. Local copies in `converted/web/sdg26_*.md`.

## Where the goals stand
- UN, The Sustainable Development Goals Report 2026, press release of 7 July 2026 (primary, `sdg26_un_press.md`): of the 139 targets with trend data, 36% are on track or making moderate progress, 49% are advancing too slowly, 15% have regressed below 2015 baselines. One in ten people in extreme poverty; about 2.3 billion face moderate or severe food insecurity; official development assistance fell a record 23.1% in 2025; financing gap about 4 trillion dollars a year. What the UN credits for a decade of gains: investment, policy, cooperation and "the data revolution" (data for nearly every indicator). AI appears once, in a list: "harnessing frontier technologies including artificial intelligence for sustainable development".

## What AI could do, by expert opinion
- Vinuesa et al. 2020, Nature Communications, doi 10.1038/s41467-019-14108-y (abstract read through OpenAlex; about 4,450 citations): by a consensus-based expert elicitation, AI "can enable the accomplishment of 134 targets across all the goals, but it may also inhibit 59 targets" (of 169: about 79% and 35%). An expert opinion exercise, not a measurement.
- Nature 2026, "Steering open-source AI to accelerate the sustainable development goals" (`sdg26_nature_os.md`): the impact of open-source AI on the SDGs has a "complex and dualistic nature"; there is no standardized quantitative framework for measuring AI's environmental impact.
- Finding: no source read measures AI's contribution to progress on any SDG indicator. The speaker's hypothesis ("may be none") stands as "not measured", which is different from "none".

## Named deployments with outcomes
- Google, "Towards a world where no one is surprised by a natural disaster", June 2026 (primary, vendor, `sdg26_flood_blog.md`): flood forecasts "cover 2 billion people across more than 150 countries"; started with a pilot in Patna, India, in 2018. GiveDirectly and the International Rescue Committee used the forecasts in Nigeria and Bangladesh to send emergency cash before the water arrived. The US National Hurricane Center used the WeatherNext model during Hurricane Melissa.
- Nature 2026, "Operational tropical cyclone forecasting with AI" (primary, Google DeepMind authors, `sdg26_cyclone.md`): WeatherNext Cyclones evaluated on 2023-2025; ensemble mean significantly more accurate than ECMWF's ENS on track and than the specialized HAFS model on intensity; public real-time forecasts since June 2025. Tropical cyclones caused more than 700,000 deaths and 1.4 trillion dollars of losses in 50 years.
- AlphaFold, Kellogg Insight 2026 on the study by Ryan Hill (Kellogg) and Carolyn Stein (Berkeley) (secondary, `sdg26_kellogg.md`): AlphaFold2 (2020) predicted structures for more than 200 million proteins, a 1,500-fold increase over decades of lab work; a lab structure cost about 100,000 dollars and years; lead developers won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. The measured effects: papers using experimental methods did not decline and still appear in top journals; work on previously unsolved proteins rose sharply ("floodlight" effect); no significant effect yet on drug development. Hill: "Because there are so many bottlenecks, there is no single task that you could fully automate that would have any meaningful impact on the rate of drug discovery." Use as the survival parable: automating one step moves the bottleneck to the next.

## What AI costs the goals
- UN University (UNU-INWEH), "Environmental Cost of AI's Energy Use: Carbon, Water and Land Footprints", 3 June 2026 (primary, `sdg26_unu.md`): data centres used about 448 TWh in 2025 (11th largest consumer if a country) and are projected at 945 TWh by 2030, nearly triple the combined electricity use of Pakistan, Bangladesh and Nigeria (650 million people); water footprint equal to the basic domestic water needs of all 1.3 billion people in sub-Saharan Africa. Inference is 80 to 90% of AI energy use. Ireland: data centres 21% of metered electricity in 2023; new approvals around Dublin paused until 2028. Only 32 countries host AI-specialised data centres and 90% of that capacity is in two countries; more than 150 countries have little or no sovereign AI compute. Up to 2.5 million tonnes of e-waste a year by 2030. Madani: "This report is not a case against artificial intelligence".

## The brighter side: faster discovery, more IT demand (added 2026-11-18)
- Gartner, "Gartner Forecasts Worldwide IT Spending to Grow 15% in 2027, Totaling $7.35 Trillion", Business Wire, 21 October 2026 (primary, analyst firm; `converted/web/innov26_gartner_it.md`): 2026 / 2027 spending, billions of dollars, growth: data centre systems 879 (+73.8%) / 1,366 (+55.5%); devices 847 / 916 (+8.1%); software 1,464 (+15.2%) / 1,679 (+14.6%); services 1,557 (+4.4%) / 1,633 (+4.9%); communications 1,352 / 1,391 (+2.9%); overall 6,391 (+14.5%) / 7,352 (+15.0%). IT services grow least: the money goes to infrastructure and software, not hours.
- Gartner AI spending, November 2026 (secondary, `innov26_innov1.md`): total AI spending 4.05 trillion dollars in 2027 (+47.5%); AI agents and assistants 30 to 67 billion; AI cybersecurity 51 to 86 billion.
- WEF Future of Jobs 2025 (primary, already in gaps_labour.md): 170 million jobs created, 92 million displaced by 2030; fastest-growing include big data specialists, fintech engineers, AI and machine learning specialists, software and application developers.
- Science: Scientific American, February 2026, citing Terence Tao's tracker: AI tools helped move about 100 Erdős problems to "solved" since October 2025, mostly "a kind of souped-up literature search" (secondary). Google DeepMind, May 2026: an agent "autonomously resolved 9 of 353 open Erdős problems at the per-problem cost of a few hundred dollars" (as quoted in Quanta). AlphaFold: research on previously unsolved proteins rose sharply (Kellogg Insight on Hill and Stein). Tropical cyclones: WeatherNext Cyclones more accurate than ECMWF ENS on track and HAFS on intensity (Nature 2026, vendor authors).
- Stanford HAI, "How AI Is Accelerating Scientific Discovery", July 2026 (`innov26_innov2.md`): Evo 2, a 40-billion-parameter DNA model; a human-cell foundation model in development. Programme descriptions, not measured outcomes.
- Counter-evidence: AI Frontiers (secondary, in analysts_adoption_hype.md): no clear inflection in patents per researcher, drug approval timelines not shortened; historical lags about 40 years for electrification and 20 for computerisation. AlphaFold: no measurable effect on drug development yet.
