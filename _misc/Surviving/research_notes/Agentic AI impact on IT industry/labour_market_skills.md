Verdict on the speaker's hypotheses, in one paragraph each (evidence is in the sections):

- (a) "Many IT specialists were laid off and AI is a cause": half supported. Layoffs are real and 2026 is worse than 2025 for tech (layoffs.fyi: 128.5k by 10 Sept 2026 vs about 123k in all of 2025; Challenger: tech cuts 149k Jan-Jul 2026, +67% y/y). AI is the reason employers state for about 20% of all announced US cuts in Jan-Oct 2026 (121.4k), but that is a stated reason, not a measured cause, and the AI share collapsed from 40% of cuts in May to under 3% in October. Independent labour data (Yale Budget Lab, EIG, Anthropic, Oxford Economics) do not show AI-driven unemployment at economy scale. The solid effect is on hiring of juniors, not on firing.
- (b) "AI killed design, copywriting, translation": overstated as "killed", supported as "badly hurt at the commodity end". Freelance platform studies show demand for substitutable writing and translation down 20-50% against trend; a third of UK translators and a quarter of illustrators report lost work. SignalFire 2026 shows design hiring at big tech at -48% vs 2019 against -11% for engineering.
- (c) Jevons-type open question: correctly framed as open. Evidence on both sides is thin and mostly opinion; the best hard numbers are app releases (+60% in Q1 2026) and software job postings recovering (+24% y/y, still 24% below Feb 2020), with 71% of the net posting growth in senior roles.
- (d) "Average coder disappears, value moves to people skills, breadth, domain depth, validation": directionally supported by BLS (programmers -6%, developers +15% for 2024-34), Dallas Fed (experience premium rising with AI exposure), Stanford (codified vs tacit knowledge), PwC, Microsoft WTI (quality control of AI output 50%, critical thinking 46%), forward deployed engineer postings (+1,000% y/y). "Understanding of human and AI biases" as a named skill has no direct data; the nearest evidence is the demand for output validation and the skill-formation RCT.
- (e) Acceleration with cognitive load and exhaustion: supported by one eight-month field study (Berkeley/HBR), one survey (BCG, n=1,488), practitioner testimony and METR's own measurement problems with multi-agent users. No controlled study of multi-agent supervision load was found.

## 1. Layoffs: counts 2022-2026, share attributed to AI, named cases, AI washing, reversals

### Takeaway
Tech layoffs in 2026 exceed 2025 but remain far below the 2023 peak, and "AI" is the stated reason for roughly a fifth of announced US job cuts in 2026, concentrated in tech and in March-July. The stated reason is not a verified cause: economists, Challenger itself, Forrester and even Sam Altman describe part of it as relabelled cost cutting, and several documented reversals exist (Klarna, Block rehires, Gartner and Forrester rehiring forecasts).

### Cited Findings

Counts (layoffs.fyi, a crowd-sourced tracker built from media reports and company announcements; it misses undisclosed and small cuts and has no commercial stake in the direction of the number):
- Full-year tech layoffs: 2023 about 265,660; 2024 about 152,922; 2025 about 122,606 at 278 companies (Computerworld gives 123,941 at 269 companies); 2026 year to 10 September: 128,536 at 299 companies. All secondhand from press coverage of layoffs.fyi; no November 2026 figure was found ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/tech-layoffs-in-2026-have-already-overtaken-last-years-toll-data-shows-123305-tech-jobs-cut-so-far), [Computerworld timeline](https://www.computerworld.com/article/3816579/tech-layoffs-this-year-a-timeline.html), [Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/2026-tech-layoffs-already-exceed-the-2025-total/)).
- Q1 comparison: Q1 2026 70,474; Q1 2025 29,845; Q1 2024 57,269. 2026 passed the 2025 full-year total by early August. A full-year 2026 projection of about 210,000 circulated in August (projection, not data). Crunchbase reported June-August 2026 layoffs down 16.2% y/y, a slowdown ([Futurism](https://futurism.com/future-society/2026-tech-layoffs-surpass-2025), [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/?p=94113)).

Challenger, Gray & Christmas (outplacement firm; it sells services to firms doing layoffs and gains visibility from the monthly report; the count records the reason the employer states in the announcement):
- July 2026 report (primary): 33,429 cuts in July; 477,033 year to date, down 41% from 806,383 in Jan-Jul 2025. AI-attributed: 10,970 in July (33%), leading reason for a fifth straight month; 112,713 year to date (about 24%); 184,538 since 2023. Technology sector: 149,023 year to date, up 67% from 89,251, and 31% of all cuts. Announced hiring plans 107,500 year to date, +25%. Andy Challenger: "AI is shifting the labor market, it is not dismantling it" and "Naming AI in a layoff announcement can win over investors while pushing current and prospective employees away" ([Challenger report, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.challengergray.com/?p=28706)).
- Through October 2026: 617,189 total cuts, down 44% y/y; AI-attributed 121,401 (about 20%), against 54,836 for all of 2025. October: 43,994 cuts (down 71% from 153,074 in Oct 2025), of which AI only 1,265 (under 3%, the lowest month of 2026); tech 5,576. "Market and economic conditions" (130,531 year to date) overtook AI as the leading 2026 reason ([HCAMag](https://www.hcamag.com/us/specialization/transformation/ai-blamed-for-121401-job-cuts-but-the-labor-data-isnt-buying-it/594129), [TheStreet Pro](https://pro.thestreet.com/market-commentary/october-job-cuts-at-3-year-low-as-focus-turns-to-economic-conditions)).
- Monthly AI-attributed cuts 2026 (derived by HCAMag from cumulative totals): Jan 7,624; Feb 4,680; Mar 15,341; Apr 21,490; May 38,579 (about 40% of the month); Jun 14,029; Jul 10,970; Aug 3,462; Sep 3,961; Oct 1,265 ([HCAMag](https://www.hcamag.com/us/specialization/transformation/ai-blamed-for-121401-job-cuts-but-the-labor-data-isnt-buying-it/594129)).
- For scale in 2025: about 55,000 AI-attributed cuts in the first 11 months of 2025 were 4.5% of all reported cuts, against 245,000 attributed to "market and economic conditions" (Challenger data as cited by Oxford Economics, in [Fortune, 2 Feb 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/02/ai-labor-market-yale-budget-lab-ai-washing/)).

- Amazon: 16,000 corporate roles cut in January 2026, more than 30,000 since October 2025; Amazon attributed the cuts to reducing bureaucracy and layers, not to technology ([Fortune, 2 Feb 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/02/ai-labor-market-yale-budget-lab-ai-washing/)).

AI washing and other causes:
- Sam Altman (OpenAI, a vendor with an interest in AI being seen as capable) said in February 2026 that some firms "are blaming AI for layoffs that they would otherwise do" ([Fortune, 10 Feb 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/10/ai-washing-and-forever-layoffs-why-companies-keep-cutting-jobs-even-amid-rising-profits/)).

- Forrester (analyst firm selling advisory): defines AI washing as attributing "financially motivated cuts" to AI; finds computer and maths roles are a small share of likely automation redundancies, with office administration, sales and finance more exposed; predicts over half of AI-attributed layoffs will be quietly reversed ([LeadDev, 26 Mar 2026](https://leaddev.com/ai/the-latest-rounds-of-tech-layoffs-have-all-the-hallmarks-of-ai-washing)).

- SignalFire (venture firm) reads 2026 layoffs such as Cisco's (about 4,000 jobs despite record quarterly revenue) as capital redirected to AI infrastructure plus a purge of zero-interest-rate-era overbuild ([SignalFire 2026](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2026)).
- US Section 174: from tax year 2022 firms had to amortise domestic R&D (including developer salaries) over 5 years and foreign R&D over 15, instead of deducting it at once; Public Law 119-21 (4 July 2025) restored immediate expensing for domestic R&D through a new Section 174A while keeping 15-year amortisation for foreign R&D. The link to layoffs is argued, not demonstrated: The Pragmatic Engineer called it "lots of speculation" ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/section-174/), [Quartz](https://qz.com/tech-layoffs-tax-code-trump-section-174-microsoft-meta-1851783502), [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/startups/r-d-tax-change-reversal-startups/)).
- Wikipedia's stub on the 2026 corporate layoffs lists other attributed causes: tariffs, H-1B substitution, offshoring ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_corporate_mass_layoffs)).

Reversal and regret:
- Gartner (analyst firm): by 2029, 30% of employees laid off in AI-driven cuts will need to be rehired, usually at higher cost; in February 2026 it predicted half of companies that cut customer service staff because of AI will rehire by 2027; of customer service AI use cases it evaluated, about a quarter had negative returns, a quarter positive, 42% unclear; median AI spend by service teams almost USD 6m in 2025 ([Customer Experience Dive, 11 Sep 2026](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/ai-driven-customer-service-cuts-gartner-predicts-rehiring/830114/)).
- Orgvue (workforce-planning software vendor, interest in selling planning tools): 39% of leaders said they had cut staff because of AI and 55% of those admitted the redundancy decisions were wrong (secondhand, [Invezz, 1 Jul 2026](https://invezz.com/news/2026/07/01/from-klarna-to-ford-why-companies-are-bringing-humans-back-after-betting-big-on-ai/)).

### Inferences
- The time profile of AI attribution (peak 38,579 in May 2026, 1,265 in October) looks more like a communication fashion than like a technology diffusion curve; a capability shock would not fall 97% in five months. This supports reading a large part of it as framing.
- Three non-AI drivers recur in every serious source: correction of 2021-22 over-hiring, the end of zero interest rates, and reallocation of cash to AI capital expenditure. The last one is an AI effect, but through budgets and not through automation of the work.
- For the talk, the honest line is "AI is the stated reason for about one announced cut in five in the US in 2026, and nobody can yet show how many of those would have happened anyway".

## 2. Entry-level work: is the junior rung being removed?

### Takeaway
The best-documented labour effect so far is fewer young people entering AI-exposed occupations: Stanford's ADP data show 22-25 year olds in the most exposed jobs 19% below less-exposed peers by June 2026 (15% a year earlier), and the Cleveland Fed, Dallas Fed and Harvard resume data point the same way, through slower hiring and not through dismissals. All of these are descriptive, the authors decline to call them causal, and economy-wide unemployment data (Yale, EIG, Anthropic, IZA, CESifo) still show no clear AI signal.

### Cited Findings

Evidence of an effect:
- Stanford Digital Economy Lab, "Canaries in the Coal Mine?", revised 12 Aug 2026 (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen; ADP payroll data; academic lab, co-author Brynjolfsson has commercial AI interests): employment of 22-25 year olds in highly AI-exposed occupations is about 19% below where it would be had it tracked less-exposed peers (June 2026), up from 15% at the July 2025 vintage. In levels, Nov 2022 to June 2026: the two most-exposed quintiles fell about 11%, the three least-exposed grew about 10%. No comparable gap for experienced workers. Adjustment runs through reduced hiring; declines concentrate where AI automates, not where it augments; employment falls for young workers in codified-knowledge occupations and rises for experienced workers in tacit-knowledge ones; so far the effect is on employment, not base pay. Robustness: persists excluding tech firms and computer occupations and controlling for interest-rate exposure and remote work, and continued after rates peaked. Caveats stated by the authors: "descriptive patterns, not causal estimates"; some pre-trends before generative AI; education explains part of the gap; ADP gaps are larger than national survey benchmarks ([Stanford DEL, 12 Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/), [paper PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)).
- The first version (Aug 2025) reported a 6-16% relative decline depending on the counterfactual (as summarised in [Anthropic, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts)). ADP/Stanford dashboard: young workers in highly exposed roles contracting 3.8% a year as of April 2026 (secondhand, [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/canaries-dashboard-ai-exposed-jobs-for-young-workers-drop-38)).
- Harvard, "Generative AI as Seniority-Biased Technological Change" (Hosseini and Lichtinger, PhD students; resume and posting data for about 62 million workers at 285,000 firms, 2015-2025; adoption proxied by postings for AI integrator roles): from Q1 2023 junior employment at adopting firms fell relative to non-adopters, about 7.7% within six quarters (press figure), driven by slower hiring, concentrated in high-exposure roles, senior employment unchanged or growing; a May 2026 draft keeps the result ([Stanford DEL seminar page](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/event/seyed-m-hosseini-and-guy-lichtinger-generative-ai-as-seniority-biased-technological-change-evidence-from-u-s-resume-and-job-posting-data), [May 2026 draft](https://raw.githack.com/s-mahdihosseini/Website/b99fa2962dcc4c165bbc3c41dd283afa99c5ccfb/GenAI_SenBTC_May_2026.pdf)).
- Dallas Fed, 6 Jan 2026: young workers' employment drops in high-exposure occupations, mostly from fewer entrants; even if all of the shortfall became unemployment it would add only about 0.1 percentage point to the aggregate rate since late 2022 ([Dallas Fed](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0106)).
- Dallas Fed, 24 Feb 2026: since autumn 2022 total US employment +2.5%, computer systems design -5%, top-decile AI-exposed sectors -1%; nominal weekly wages +7.5% nationwide, +16.7% in computer systems design, +8.5% in the top-decile exposed industries. Across 205 occupations the experience premium (median 40%) correlates with AI exposure; per one standard deviation of exposure, post-2022 wage growth is -0.28 pp where the experience premium is zero and +0.2 pp at the 90th percentile of premium. Interpretation: AI substitutes for codified entry-level tasks and complements tacit knowledge ([Dallas Fed](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0224)).
- Cleveland Fed Economic Commentary 2026-26 (Cline and Kaymak; CPS, ages 23-54): against a 2015-19 baseline, unemployment rose about 1 pp more in the most-exposed than the least-exposed occupations (May 2025-Apr 2026); job-finding rates fell about 6 points more (implied unemployment spell from about 10.5 to over 13 weeks); separations show no robust pattern; for ages 23-30 the exposure gradient is about 2 pp against at most 1 pp for ages 45-54. Associations only; "an early and necessarily incomplete look" ([Cleveland Fed](https://www.clevelandfed.org/publications/economic-commentary/2026/ec-202626-job-finding-and-unemployment-in-the-age-of-ai)).
- SignalFire State of Talent 2026 (22 June 2026; venture firm, proprietary Beacon data built largely on public profiles, 12 "Tech Majors"): new-grad hiring down about 65% vs 2019 at the Tech Majors and about 76% at early-stage startups; total hiring at Tech Majors -25% vs 2019 (peak +63% in 2022). By function vs 2019 at Tech Majors: engineering -11%, product management -39%, design -48%, marketing -36%; at startups engineering +7%, design -22%. Engineering is 55% of Tech Majors hiring (46% in 2019). Top-20 CS graduates of 2025 are 45% less likely to land a Tech Major job and twice as likely to call themselves founders as the class of 2022. The report says the "AI code apocalypse" for engineers has not materialised and that "the review bottleneck has replaced the coding bottleneck" ([SignalFire 2026](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2026)). The 2025 edition: new grads 7% of Big Tech hires, new-grad hiring down over 50% vs 2019 ([SignalFire 2025](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2025%20)).
- Indeed Hiring Lab, 23 July 2026 (job site; data are postings, not employment): in software development 69.3% of postings are senior and 4.5% entry-level (Q1 2026); across the US senior postings +14.7% y/y and entry-level -7.5% y/y as of May 2026; entry-level postings peaked in 2022 ([Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/23/the-labor-market-is-tilting-toward-seniority/)).
- NY Fed, labour market for recent graduates (ages 22-27): by-major table released Feb 2026 on 2024 data shows computer engineering unemployment 7.8% (second highest of 73 majors) and computer science about 7.0% (fifth highest), with underemployment below 20% against 42% overall; one analysis puts computer engineering at 2.3% in 2022 (secondhand, [VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/tech-news/us-s-computer-engineering-grads-post-second-highest-unemployment-rate-5048192.html), [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-computer-engineering-grads-jobless-rate-rose-3-4x-to-7-8/)). Latest quarter 2026 Q3: recent-graduate unemployment 5.2% (lowest in over a year), underemployment 42.5% ([NY Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market)).

Evidence of no or small effect:
- Yale Budget Lab with Brookings (Oct 2025, monthly CPS updates into 2026; academic centre): no "discernible disruption" in occupational mix since November 2022; the March 2026 update says the data "continues to indicate no meaningful impact of AI on the labor market"; unemployment duration for high-exposure workers unchanged ([Yale Budget Lab](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/what-we-do-and-dont-know-about-how-ai-affecting-labor-market), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/02/ai-labor-market-yale-budget-lab-ai-washing/)).
- Economic Innovation Group, "AI and Jobs: The Final Word (Until the Next One)", 10 Aug 2025 (Eckhardt and Goldschlag; bipartisan think tank funded by tech entrepreneurs; CPS, five exposure measures): from 2022 to early 2025 unemployment rose 0.30 pp in the most-exposed quintile and 0.94 pp in the least-exposed; exposed workers had the lowest labour-force exit rate; for recent graduates the exposed-unexposed gap is 0.2-0.3 pp, "really quite small"; about 9% of businesses used AI in production (27% in the information sector); about 95% of firms report no net employment effect ([EIG](https://eig.org/ai-and-jobs-the-final-word/)).
- Anthropic, "Labor market impacts of AI", 5 Mar 2026 (Massenkoff and McCrory; vendor of the technology, with an interest both in AI looking capable and in it looking safe): "no systematic increase in unemployment for highly exposed workers since late 2022"; the method can detect about a 1 pp differential. Suggestive: job-finding rate of 22-25 year olds in exposed occupations down about 14% relative to 2022, marginally significant, no decline for over-25s. "Observed exposure": computer programmers 75% task coverage (highest), data entry keyers 67%; Claude covers 33% of computer and maths tasks against a theoretical 94%; each 10 points of coverage goes with 0.6 pp lower BLS projected growth 2024-34 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts)).
- IZA and CESifo papers (autumn 2026) find no statistically significant rise in relative unemployment of recent US graduates in June-August 2026 (secondhand summaries, [IZA DP 18945](https://docs.iza.org/dp18945.pdf), [Let's Data Science on CESifo](https://letsdatascience.com/news/cesifo-study-finds-no-ai-graduate-job-collapse-4398908f)).
- Oxford Economics: firms "don't appear to be replacing workers with AI on a significant scale"; Goldman Sachs estimate: AI lowered monthly payroll growth by about 16,000 over the past year and raised unemployment by 0.1 pp (secondhand, [Oxford Economics](https://www.oxfordeconomics.com/resource/evidence-of-an-ai-driven-shakeup-of-job-markets-is-patchy/), [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-jobs-ai-is-likely-to-boost-and-those-it-may-disrupt)).
- Critique of Canaries: Apollo's Torsten Slok attributes entry-level weakness to a low-hire, low-fire market ([Fortune, 27 Jun 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/27/what-is-ai-impact-entry-level-jobs-stanford-adp-canaries-brynjolfsson-richardson/)). A January 2026 EIG paper is reported to argue the decline in young hiring in exposed jobs began with monetary tightening before ChatGPT (secondhand, via a summary of a July 2026 Stanford SIEPR brief; not verified).

Firms that hire juniors on purpose:
- Shopify: from about 100 interns a year to more than 1,000 in 2025, and 1,000 planned for 2026. Farhan Thawar (head of engineering): "These folks coming out of schools now are growing up with AI"; "You have the responsibility of the code it generates once you submit it" ([CoderPad interview, 23 Apr 2026](https://coderpad.io/blog/hiring-developers/in-the-ai-era-shopify-is-investing-in-junior-engineers-not-cutting-them/); CoderPad sells hiring tools).

### Inferences
- The two bodies of evidence are compatible: a hiring slowdown concentrated in 22-25 year olds in a few occupations is too small to move aggregate unemployment (Dallas Fed: about 0.1 pp), so "no economy-wide effect" and "juniors in exposed jobs are hurt" can both be true.
- Software engineering is not the worst-hit function among juniors: SignalFire's function split puts design, marketing and product management well below engineering.
- Answer for students: the entry gate is narrower and the bar is "can own and verify output", but unemployment for CS graduates (about 7% on 2024 data) coexists with very low underemployment, meaning those who get in are still used at graduate level.

## 4. Occupations already hit: writing, translation, illustration, design, voice, support

### Takeaway
There is causal-style evidence (platform data with counterfactual trends) that demand for commodity writing and translation fell 20-50% after ChatGPT, and survey evidence that a third of translators and a quarter of illustrators lost work, so "badly hurt" is supported and "killed" is not. Demand grew for AI-complementary freelance work, and customer support is the area with the most documented over-automation and reversal.

### Cited Findings
- Teutloff, Einsiedler, Kässi, Braesemann, Mishkin, del Rio-Chanona, "Winners and losers of generative AI: Early evidence of shifts in freelancer demand", Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization 235 (2025), peer reviewed, open access (Oxford Internet Institute and partners): postings on a leading freelance platform grouped into 116 skill clusters; demand for substitutable skills such as writing and translation fell 20-50% relative to the counterfactual trend, most sharply for short (1-3 week) jobs; ML programming demand +24%; AI chatbot development nearly tripled; demand for novice workers declined in general ([Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:af491969-bee7-4b09-a696-2b500b6b21d8), [OII](https://sosi.oii.ox.ac.uk/publications/winners-and-losers-of-generative-ai-early-evidence-of-shifts-in-freelancer-demand/)). Press detail: "About us" page writing -50%, translation for Western languages -30%, other languages about -20% ([Complexity Science Hub](https://csh.ac.at/news/how-is-generative-ai-impacting-the-freelance-job-market/)).
- Demirci, Hannane and Zhu (Imperial College, DIW Berlin) summarised their freelance-platform study in HBR on 11 Nov 2024; the page with figures is paywalled ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2024/11/research-how-gen-ai-is-already-impacting-the-labor-market)).
- Society of Authors (UK writers' trade union, an interested party; survey of 787 members, January 2024): 26% of illustrators and 36% of translators said they had already lost work to generative AI; 37% of illustrators and 43% of translators said their income had fallen in value ([The Bookseller](https://thebookseller.com/news/a-third-of-translators-report-losing-work-to-generative-ai-systems-soa-survey-reveals), [European Writers' Council](https://europeanwriterscouncil.eu/soa-survey-uk-ai-2024/)).

- Design inside tech firms: design hiring at the 12 Tech Majors -48% vs 2019, at early-stage startups -22%, against -11% and +7% for engineering ([SignalFire 2026](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2026)).
- Customer support: Klarna's reversal and Gartner's rehiring forecasts (section 1); Gartner finds only about a quarter of evaluated customer-service AI use cases with positive returns ([Customer Experience Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/ai-driven-customer-service-cuts-gartner-predicts-rehiring/830114/)).

- Anthropic's observed-exposure ranking puts customer service representatives second after computer programmers ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts)).
- Historical note from Bessen (2015), before generative AI: the median graphic designer wage had been roughly stagnant for 30 years while the top 10% earned six figures, an earlier polarisation after desktop publishing ([IMF Finance and Development, Mar 2015](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/fandd/2015/03/bessen.htm)).

## 5. Jevons paradox for software and historical analogies

### Takeaway
Whether cheaper software creation expands demand faster than agents absorb the work is unresolved; the data so far show more software being shipped and a partial, senior-skewed recovery in developer postings, alongside a shrinking entry tier. The historical analogies cut both ways: ATMs coexisted with more tellers for thirty years and then a different technology removed them.

### Cited Findings
- More software: Appfigures counted 24% more new apps submitted to Apple's App Store in 2025 than in 2024, and new releases across both stores up 60% in Q1 2026 (secondhand through opinion pieces, [O'Reilly Radar](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/the-world-needs-more-software-engineers/), [Neomind Labs](https://www.neomindlabs.com/2026/06/03/jevons-paradox-comes-for-software/)).
- AI usage volume: Google went from 9.7 trillion tokens a month in May 2024 to more than 3.2 quadrillion in May 2026 (a measure of compute use, not of software employment; same commentary sources as above).
- Demand for developers: postings +23.8% y/y, +15% since Feb 2025 while all postings fell 7%, still about 24% below Feb 2020; 71% of the net gain in senior roles (section 3 sources).
- More founders: top-20 CS graduates of 2025 are twice as likely to describe themselves as founders as the class of 2022; startups' engineering hiring is +7% vs 2019 while Tech Majors' is -11% ([SignalFire 2026](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2026)).
- Counter-arguments: Jevons "only holds when demand grows faster than the cost falls"; a 70% cut in delivery effort requires more than three times as much software to keep build hours flat ([Forbes Technology Council, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbestechcouncil/2026/11/05/ai-will-make-code-cheap-but-it-wont-shrink-most-engineering-teams/); council posts are paid-membership opinion). The effect may hold in aggregate while the generic application developer "doing similar work at company after company" loses demand because end users build one-off software themselves ([Jevons Inversion, Substack](https://davidsj.substack.com/p/jevons-inversion)). BLS projects the narrow "programmer" job to decline while developers grow ([The Jevons paradox has a second half](https://hrih.si/articles/the-jevons-paradox-has-a-second-half/)).
- SignalFire: AI "collapses coordination work that once needed five or six specialists", enabling flatter organisations and "Super IC" roles; managers at Tech Majors now have about 12 engineers each against 10 in 2019 (+14%), about 15 at startups (+34%); product managers support 22% more engineers than in 2019 ([SignalFire 2026](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2026)).
- Bank tellers and ATMs (James Bessen, IMF Finance and Development, March 2015): over 400,000 ATMs installed in the US; tellers per urban branch fell from 20 to 13 between 1988 and 2004; urban branches grew 43%; remaining teller work moved to sales and relationships ([IMF F&D](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/fandd/2015/03/bessen.htm)). Commentary citing Bessen gives teller employment of about 500,000 in 1980 and nearly 600,000 in 2010 ([Conversable Economist](https://conversableeconomist.blogspot.com/2015/03/atms-and-rising-number-of-bank-tellers.html)); another reading of BLS data gives 531,000 in 1980 and 453,000 in 2010 (definitions differ).
- The second half of the teller story: BLS counts 339,200 tellers in 2025 and projects -13% (about -44,700) for 2025-35, citing branch closures and mobile and online banking ([BLS tellers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/tellers.htm)); the essay "Why the ATM didn't kill bank teller jobs, but the iPhone did" argues the decline came from a later technology that removed the need for the branch itself ([David Oks](https://davidoks.blog/p/why-the-atm-didnt-kill-bank-teller)).
- Bessen's other example: 19th-century power looms automated 98% of the labour per yard of cloth, yet weaving jobs grew because demand for cloth was elastic ([IMF F&D](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/fandd/2015/03/bessen.htm)).

### Inferences
- The teller series is a good slide for hypothesis (c) precisely because it has two phases: complementarity while demand for the surrounding service expanded, then decline when a technology removed the bottleneck the humans sat on. For software the analogous question is whether agents are the "ATM" (cheaper production, more products) or the "smartphone" (the customer no longer needs the intermediary).
- Available hard data (apps shipped, postings, founders) speak to output and vacancies, not to total developer employment; nobody has a series yet that settles the sign.

### Gaps
- Radiologists (Hinton's 2016 "stop training radiologists" and subsequent growth in radiologist numbers and pay), spreadsheets and bookkeepers versus accountants, and compilers versus programmers: no sources retrieved, the searches were cut off by the budget limit. These need a follow-up pass before going on a slide.
- Tiny-team revenue examples (Cursor, Lovable, Midjourney and similar ARR per employee), solo-founder data and US Census business applications were not retrieved.
- No econometric estimate of the price elasticity of demand for software was found.

## 6. Skills: what moves up in value

### Takeaway
Across government projections, central-bank analysis, vendor surveys and job-posting data, value is moving from producing code to specifying, verifying and owning outcomes with customers: quality control of AI output and critical thinking top Microsoft's 2026 list, experienced and tacit knowledge earns a rising premium, and the fastest-growing engineering title is a customer-facing one. Most of these sources are vendors or consultancies with something to sell, and the evidence is postings and self-reports, not measured returns to particular skills.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft Work Trend Index 2026 (published May 2026; survey of 20,000 AI-using knowledge workers in 10 countries fielded Feb-Apr 2026 by Edelman DXI, plus Microsoft 365 telemetry; Microsoft sells Copilot and agents): the most valued skills as AI takes on more work are quality control of AI output (50%) and critical thinking (46%); 86% treat AI output as a starting point, not a final answer. "Frontier Professionals" are 16% of AI users (3,233 of 20,000): advanced agent use on multi-step work, routine workflow redesign, repeatable AI practices; 80% of them say they produce work they could not have a year ago against 58% of all AI users. "Transformation Paradox": 65% fear falling behind, 45% say it is safer to focus on current goals than to redesign work, 13% say they are rewarded for reinventing work even if results fall short, 26% say leadership is consistently aligned on AI. Active agents in Microsoft 365 grew 15x y/y ([Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization)).
- Dallas Fed: the experience premium is positively correlated with AI exposure, and wages in exposed occupations grow faster where experience matters more; firms will "need new ways for entry-level workers to gain experience" ([Dallas Fed, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0224)). Stanford: codified-knowledge occupations lose young workers, tacit-knowledge ones gain experienced workers ([Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)).
- Forward deployed engineer (customer-embedded engineer, Palantir's model): postings +1,000% in Jan-Aug 2026 vs the same period of 2025 and +4,600% vs 2023 (Lightcast), while tech postings overall grew 13%; Indeed-based count 643 postings in April 2025 to 5,330 in April 2026 (+729%). Required: programming and ML plus consulting, communication, working on-site with client executives, understanding the client's workflows and data; "being able to connect that technical knowledge to a business problem" (Dice). Hiring: Palantir, Microsoft, Meta, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic ([Fortune, 3 Sep 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/09/03/forward-deployed-engineers-fast-growing-six-figure-silicon-valley-job-integrate-ai-with-customers-tech-careers-palantir/), [Paraform](https://www.paraform.com/insights/forward-deployed-engineer-demand-quadrupled)). Base is small: one tracker counted 134 active FDE postings at 69 companies on 30 Apr 2026 ([FDE Pulse](https://fdepulse-com.pages.dev/insights/fde-hiring-trends-2026/)).
- SignalFire 2026 role shifts within engineering: AI/ML share +39% and research +28% since 2022, forward-deployed +30%, sales engineers about +11%, security +3%, front-end about -25% (back-end engineers now build front ends with generative tools) ([SignalFire 2026](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2026)).
- Product-engineer convergence: Shopify reports managers, directors, VPs and the CEO contributing working code regularly with AI tools ([CoderPad, 23 Apr 2026](https://coderpad.io/blog/hiring-developers/in-the-ai-era-shopify-is-investing-in-junior-engineers-not-cutting-them/)).
- Stack Overflow (developer Q&A site whose traffic is threatened by AI; pulse survey reported 16 Mar 2026): daily AI use at work 58% (47% in 2025); by career stage 68% early, 59% mid, 56% experienced; 64% use AI to learn (44% in 2025, 37% in 2024); 38% cite lack of trust in results as a barrier; experienced developers are the most distrustful; only 1% learn with AI alone. The piece is titled "Domain expertise still wanted" ([Stack Overflow blog](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/03/16/domain-expertise-still-wanted-the-latest-trends-in-ai/)).
- Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026 (30,903 responses, 169 countries): 48.0% trust AI when the output is easy to verify; 6.6% trust it with important decisions; 78.7% say source attribution matters (secondhand, [IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/stack-overflow-survey-shows-developers-trust-ai-with-caveats)).
- Skill formation (Anthropic RCT, 29 Jan 2026, arXiv 2601.20245; 52 mostly junior engineers learning the Trio library): the AI-assisted group scored 50% on a follow-up quiz against 67% for the hand-coding group (p = 0.01), with the largest gap on debugging; the speed gain (about 23 vs 25 minutes) was not significant (p = 0.391). Participants who asked conceptual questions or requested explanations scored 65% or more; delegation and iterative AI debugging patterns scored below 40%. Small sample, immediate quiz, sidebar assistant and not agents; published by a vendor ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)).
- PwC 2026 Barometer headline: "human skills on top", AI-skill wage premium 62% ([PwC Australia](https://www.pwc.com.au/media/2026/2026-AI-Jobs-Barometer.html)).
- Forrester's Michael O'Grady: "most of the jobs that are influenced by AI would be augmented rather than displaced" ([LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/ai/the-latest-rounds-of-tech-layoffs-have-all-the-hallmarks-of-ai-washing)).

### Inferences
- The speaker's list in (d) maps onto evidence as follows. People skills and customer proximity: FDE postings and pay, PwC "human skills". Deep domain expertise to validate output: Microsoft's top two skills, Stack Overflow trust figures (6.6% trust AI with important decisions), Dallas Fed tacit knowledge. Breadth: back-end engineers absorbing front-end, PMs and executives committing code, "Super IC". Management: SignalFire shows the opposite for people management (flatter organisations, wider spans), so "management" should be read as managing agents and outcomes, not as more manager jobs.
- The skill-formation RCT gives students a concrete rule: using AI to ask "why" preserved learning, using it to delegate did not. It also explains why firms fear a missing generation of seniors.

### Gaps
- WEF Future of Jobs 2025 could not be fetched (403). Recalled headline figures (170 million jobs created, 92 million displaced by 2030, 39% of core skills changing, about 40% of employers expecting to cut staff where AI automates tasks) are unverified in this session.
- No McKinsey, BCG skills reports or LinkedIn "skills on the rise" data were retrieved.
- No data source was found that names "understanding human and AI biases" as a demanded skill; this part of the hypothesis rests on inference from validation demand.
- Microsoft's "agent boss" wording comes from the 2025 edition, which was not re-read.

## 7. Working conditions: acceleration, cognitive load, switching, exhaustion, deskilling

### Takeaway
Field and survey evidence from 2026 agrees with hypothesis (e): AI use raises pace and scope, creates several parallel threads and constant checking of agent output, and a minority report acute mental fatigue; the evidence base is one ethnographic study, one consultancy survey and practitioner testimony, with no controlled measurement of multi-agent supervision yet. Measured productivity gains for experienced developers remain uncertain, and one randomised trial shows a learning cost.

### Cited Findings
- Ranganathan and Ye (UC Berkeley Haas), "AI Doesn't Reduce Work, It Intensifies It", HBR, 9 Feb 2026: eight-month study of about 200 employees at a US technology company. AI use led people to work faster, take on a broader scope and extend hours ("workload creep"). Quoted passages: "AI introduced a new rhythm in which workers managed several active threads at once", including "running multiple agents in parallel" and "reviving long-deferred tasks because AI could 'handle them' in the background"; "the reality was a continual switching of attention, frequent checking of AI outputs, and a growing number of open tasks. This created cognitive load and a sense of always juggling, even as the work felt productive"; this can "make it harder for organizations to distinguish genuine productivity gains from unsustainable intensity". Proposed remedy: a shared "AI practice" ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/02/ai-doesnt-reduce-work-it-intensifies-it), excerpts via [Simon Willison, 9 Feb 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/9/ai-intensifies-work/), [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/business/business-strategy/ai-isnt-making-work-easier-its-intensifying-it-researchers-say-teams-are-now-facing-unsustainable-workloads-cognitive-strain-and-higher-levels-of-burnout)). Single firm, qualitative.
- Practitioner testimony, Simon Willison (independent developer and heavy agent user): "the productivity boost these things can provide is exhausting"; "I'm frequently finding myself with work on two or three projects running parallel"; "after just an hour or two my mental energy for the day feels almost entirely depleted"; people are "losing sleep" over "just one more prompt" ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/9/ai-intensifies-work/)).
- BCG "AI brain fry" (Julie Bedard and colleagues, HBR, March 2026; survey of 1,488 full-time US workers; consultancy): about 14% reported the condition, highest in marketing (25.9%) and HR (19.3%); tasks needing more oversight of AI output were associated with 14% more mental effort, 12% more mental fatigue and 19% more information overload; self-reported productivity was higher with three or fewer AI tools and fell with four or more; 34% of affected workers showed active intent to leave against 25% of others; manager training and support reduced it ([Fortune, 10 Mar 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/03/10/ai-brain-fry-workplace-productivity-bcg-study/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/study-warns-of-ai-brain-fry-as-workers-hit-cognitive-limits-overseeing-ai-agents/), [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/concerns-are-mounting-over-the-cognitive-impact-of-ai-as-workers-report-experiencing-brain-fry-and-its-causing-increased-employee-errors-decision-fatigue-and-intention-to-quit)). The 14% share and function split are from secondary coverage.
- "Workslop" (BetterUp Labs with Stanford Social Media Lab, HBR, Sept 2025; 1,150 US desk workers; BetterUp sells coaching): 40% received low-effort AI-generated work in the past month (54% of managers); each instance costs just under two hours to deal with; about USD 186 per employee per month, about USD 9 million a year for a 10,000-person firm (derived from self-reported time and salaries) ([BetterUp](https://www.betterup.com/blog/hidden-costs-workslop), [CNBC, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2025/09/23/ai-generated-workslop-is-destroying-productivity-and-teams-researchers-say.html)).
- METR (non-profit evaluator): the 2025 randomised trial found 16 experienced open-source developers took 19% longer with AI (interval +2% to +39%) on 246 real issues. Update of 24 Feb 2026 (57 developers, 800+ tasks, 143 repositories): estimated change in task time -18% for 10 returning developers (interval -38% to +9%) and -4% for 47 new ones (-15% to +9%), neither distinguishable from zero. METR calls the data an "unreliable signal": 30-50% of developers withheld tasks they did not want to do without AI, and time measurement is "unreliable for the fraction of developers who use multiple AI agents concurrently", who work on unrelated tasks while waiting for an agent. METR believes developers are more sped up in early 2026 than in early 2025 ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)). A May 2026 METR survey of 349 technical workers gives a self-reported median 3x speed-up (secondhand, [devs-group](https://devs-group.ch/en/blog/ai-productivity-studies-2026/)).
- Review burden: SignalFire states that "the review bottleneck has replaced the coding bottleneck" ([SignalFire 2026](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2026)); Stack Overflow describes an "AI tax" where some users report no time saved and open-source projects resist AI-assisted pull requests ([Stack Overflow blog](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/03/16/domain-expertise-still-wanted-the-latest-trends-in-ai/)).
- Pressure: 65% of AI-using knowledge workers fear falling behind if they do not adapt quickly ([Microsoft WTI 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization)).
- Skill atrophy: the Anthropic RCT (50% vs 67% quiz scores, largest gap in debugging) in section 6 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)).

### Inferences
- METR's remark is independent corroboration of the speaker's own observation: the waiting-and-switching pattern is common enough among agent users to break a time-tracking study design.
- Two different loads appear in the evidence: oversight load (reviewing output, BCG's +12% fatigue) and switching load (several open threads, Berkeley). Agents reduce typing and increase both.
- The honest summary on productivity: self-reports say large gains (median 3x), the only randomised trials on experienced developers say "between slower and modestly faster, not statistically clear", and organisations cannot yet tell real gains from unsustainable intensity.

### Gaps
- Upwork Research Institute burnout surveys (2024 and 2025) could not be retrieved; recalled figures (2024: 77% of employees said AI tools added to their workload) are unverified here.
- No controlled study measuring cognitive load as a function of the number of agents supervised was found; the "three tools" threshold from BCG concerns tools, not concurrent agents.
- The Microsoft Research and Carnegie Mellon study on generative AI and critical thinking, DORA 2026, and any 2026 burnout data specific to developers were not retrieved.
- The HBR articles themselves are paywalled; quotes are via secondary excerpts.
