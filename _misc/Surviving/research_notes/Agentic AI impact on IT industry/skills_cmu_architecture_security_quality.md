# What universities and employers now say students need: CMU, Microsoft, architecture, security, quality

Added 2026-11-18 at the speaker's request. Local copies in `converted/web/skills26_*.md`. All read in full unless stated.

## Carnegie Mellon and Microsoft: PRAISE
- Microsoft, "What will the next generation of developers do when AI writes the code?", October 2026 (primary, employer; `skills26_ms_nextgen.md`): PRAISE, Preceptorship for AI-accelerated Software Engineering, pairs junior engineers with senior "preceptors", borrowed from nursing. "More of our time is now spent on design and architecture than coding every line. But that doesn't change the need for us to understand what the underlying code does". Preceptors build "strong systems thinking, taste, and judgement". "AI changes the work; it does not remove human responsibility for outcomes." Juniors should "grow from producing code to deciding what deserves to be built in the first place." CMU's Michael Hilton: "It used to be that early-career software engineers would do a lot of tasks that would build up their expertise".
- CMU School of Computer Science news on PRAISE (primary, `skills26_cmu_praise.md`): the idea came from Mark Russinovich (Azure CTO, CMU alumnus) noting that AI tools help senior engineers but not juniors. Courses: Machine Learning in Production; Preceptorship in AI-Augmented Software Engineering I and II; DevOps: Engineering for Secure Development and Deployment. Credits count towards a master's in software engineering.
- Satya Nadella, The Pragmatic Engineer podcast, via Business Insider 17 November 2026 (secondary, `skills26_nadella_bi.md`): "In a world where code generation has become cheap, what has become hard is verification." "There's an entire new way to build that is emerging, and it's new software engineering. And it requires software engineers." Advice: be on teams that build, hands-on.

## CMU teaching
- CMU News, "Navigating the AI Era with a CMU Focus on Critical Thinking", 27 August 2026 (primary, `skills26_cmu_crit2.md`): Tom Cortina (associate dean, SCS): students "have to know less about writing a lot of code from scratch, but they need to know how computer code works"; "They will work at a higher level, doing things like planning and designing software"; "it still needs to be the student's program, even though AI has created some of the code". Assignments now include more tests and code reviews, and TAs interview students about their projects. Zico Kolter: "We need to find a way to teach effectively in a world where students have AI at their disposal to basically solve any homework problem." Mike Taylor's course "Effective Coding With AI": "the students who learned the most were the ones who refused to let the AI think for them"; student best practices included "verify everything", "work incrementally" and "use multiple models deliberately".
- Communications of the ACM, "The AI Deskilling Paradox", November 2025 (`skills26_cacm_deskill.md`): Aniket Kittur (CMU HCII) on deskilling; Matt Beane on apprenticeship loss.
- Communications of the ACM, "The Outlook for Computer Science Education", April 2026 (`skills26_cacm_outlook.md`): US CS undergraduate enrolment down nearly 6% in fall 2025 while all undergraduate enrolment rose 2.4%; CS graduates' unemployment 6.1% in 2025 (New York Fed), starting salary about 80,000 dollars.

## Quality, architecture, security
- QA Financial, June 2026, JPMorgan Chase CIO Gill Haus (secondary, `skills26_jpm_test.md`): AI coding automates much of coding; "engineering discipline, automated testing and digital resilience are becoming more important, not less"; faster code generation "increases the need for robust testing, governance and architectural discipline".
- Already in the research: BLS 2025-2035 information security analysts +21%; security share of tech-firm engineering roles +3% (SignalFire); the PHP2Shell case (finding cheap, fixing not); Veracode secure-code pass rate 56% (July 2026) to 62% (October 2026), so about 38% of AI-generated code samples still vulnerable (I4/I8); CVE records doubled in 2026 (I8.1).
- No labour statistic separates "software architect" from developers. Architecture appears as the stated shift of senior work (Microsoft, CMU, JPMorgan), not as a counted job category.
