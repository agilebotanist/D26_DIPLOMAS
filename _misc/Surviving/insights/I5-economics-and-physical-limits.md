# I5. Economics and physical limits

Theme file for the talk "Agentic AI impact on the IT industry". Synthesis of the research notes as of 2026-11-17, not new research. Backstage material: items 2, 3, 5 and 6 of each insight are for speaker notes, backup slides and questions. Item 9 is what may reach a slide.

## Summary in five lines

1. The one thing to remember: the price of a given level of machine intelligence falls about 13 times a year, and the bill for agentic work still goes up, because the newest tier holds its price and agents consume far more of it.
2. The best story: Uber told its engineers to use AI as much as possible, spent its annual AI budget in four months, and then capped each employee at 1,500 dollars a month per coding tool.
3. The best picture: two bars for the Stargate project in April 2026, about 0.3 GW running against more than 9 GW planned, next to a laptop price tag that is 14% higher because of memory.
4. The best question for discussion: if yesterday's intelligence gets 13 times cheaper every year, why did the average company in one survey spend 4.7 times last year's amount when it had planned 1.9 times?
5. What the speaker should not claim: that tokens are getting more expensive, that the build-out is a fraud or a bubble about to burst, that vendor numbers are false, or anything about Nvidia's share price and management (unverified, see Open points). No valuation judgements and no investment advice.

How to read the verification levels: "primary", "secondary", "search-result only", "not opened" and "not stated" are the levels recorded in `references_keymap.tsv`. A quote marked EXACT was found as a contiguous match by `verify_quotes.py` in the local converted file named in the quote log at the end.

## I5.1 Tokens get cheaper every quarter, the task does not

1. Title as a claim. Tokens get cheaper every quarter, the task does not.

2. Number or data series.
   - Price of a fixed capability, Epoch AI, September 2026 (primary): about 47% lower per quarter, or 13 times per year, averaged over five benchmarks and three years. When a capability level is new the fall is about 75 times per year, two years later 4.7 times per year. Example: about 0.30 dollars per GPQA Diamond question at the 75% level in January 2025, 0.0004 dollars under 18 months later [@epochai2026plunging; @epochai2026substack].
   - Older Epoch series for a log-scale chart, dollars per 1M tokens (primary): GPT-3 level on MMLU 60.00 (2021-11), 20.00 (2022-09), 2.00 (2023-03), 0.10 (2024-07), 0.07 (2024-10). GPT-4 level on MMLU 37.50 (2023-03), 0.18 (2025-02) [@epochai2025insightllm].
   - Output list prices on 2026-11-17, dollars per 1M tokens, vendors' own pages (primary): GPT-6 Astra 50; Claude Fable 5.2 40; Claude Opus 5.5 20; Kimi K3 15; Gemini 4 Carbon 10 (20 from 2026-12-01); GPT-6.1 Sol 10; Claude Sonnet 5.5 10; DeepSeek V4 Pro 3.96 peak; DeepSeek Flash 1.20 peak; GPT-6 Luna 0.50; Claude Haiku 5.5 0.50 [@openai2026apipricing; @anthropic2026apipricing; @google2026gemini; @moonshotai2026model; @deepseek2026models]. The spread between the dearest and the cheapest current model is 100-fold.
   - Top tier on one vendor's list over time (primary): retired Opus 4 and 4.1 at 75; Opus 4.5 to 5 at 25; Opus 5.5 at 20; a new tier above it, Fable 5 and 5.1, at 50; Fable 5.2 at 40 [@anthropic2026apipricing].
   - Cost per benchmark task against score, Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0.1, read 2026-11-17 (primary): Fable 5.2 score 65, 26.54 dollars; Gemini 4 Carbon 60, 5.48; Opus 5.5 59, 10.04; GPT-6 Astra 57, 6.24; GPT-6.1 Sol 55, 1.27; Kimi K3.1 Pro 46, 3.68 [@artificialanalysis2026comparison].
   - Slide number, if any: the 100-fold spread of list prices. The pair 1.27 and 26.54 dollars for the same benchmark task is the backup.

3. Quotes.
   - "the price for a given level of performance has fallen about 47% per quarter, or 13× per year" [@epochai2026plunging]. Speaker: Epoch AI report text. Role and interest: non-profit research institute, no product to sell, an interest in measuring AI progress. Result: EXACT (Q1).
   - "when a performance level is first achieved, AI companies can briefly charge a premium for it, before competition and technological improvement quickly drive down the price" [@epochai2026plunging]. Same source. Result: EXACT (Q2).

4. Example or case. GPT-6 Astra has the highest list price on the table (50 dollars) and is still cheaper per benchmark task than Opus 5.5 (6.24 against 10.04 dollars), because it used about a quarter of the output tokens (54M against 218M). The top model costs about 21 times as much per task as GPT-6.1 Sol for 10 more index points. A second case, current and primary: Google's price page announces that Gemini 4 Carbon doubles from 2.00 and 10.00 to 4.00 and 20.00 dollars on 2026-12-01 [@google2026gemini].

5. Counter-evidence, in proportion. Prices also fall at the top: Menlo notes that "in recent weeks, Anthropic and OpenAI have started discounting even their flagship models" (EXACT, Q3) [@menlo2026stateenterpriseai], and TechCrunch reports September 2026 cuts by both (secondary, no bib key yet). Per-task rankings do not follow per-token rankings, so a dear model can be the cheap choice for a task. A benchmark task is not a unit of real work, and Epoch itself lists benchmark-specific training and noisy data as caveats.

6. Speaker-note material. Corrections from later notes: the earlier file gave an 80-fold spread on Anthropic's own list (0.50 against 40 dollars); the later cross-vendor table gives 100-fold (0.50 against 50). The earlier Epoch range of 9 to 900 times per year (2025 data insight) is replaced as the headline by the 2026 report's average of 13 times per year. The earlier cost-per-task figures from a secondary blog are replaced by Artificial Analysis pages read directly. A widely repeated line that per-token prices fell 75% while per-task costs rose 233%, attributed to Gartner, has no traceable origin and must not be used. Interests: Artificial Analysis sells benchmark data to the industry; a16z, which published the similar estimate of about 10 times a year, is a venture investor with AI holdings [@appenzeller2024llmflation]; price lists are facts as of the read date and carry no date of their own.

7. Confidence and thesis. High for list prices and the Epoch trend (primary). Medium for cost per task (one provider, one index). Corrects T6: frontier models do not simply "grow expensive"; a fixed capability gets cheap fast, the newest tier holds its price, and tasks use more. Supports the token-cost part of T14.

8. Question for the audience. Which of your tasks really need the model that costs 100 times more, and who in your team decides that?

9. Personal angle. The brief gives the speaker's daily use for office, admin, teaching and research work and "no way back" (T17). Plain sentence without a number: "What I used last year is almost free today, and I still pay more, because I no longer want last year's model." Speaker to supply: a moment when he chose between a cheap and a dear model for a real piece of work, or decided the dear one was not worth it.

## I5.2 The flat-rate plan was a subsidy, and the bill has moved to the buyer

1. Title as a claim. The flat-rate plan was a subsidy, and the bill has moved to the buyer.

2. Number or data series.
   - Three withdrawals of flat plans. Cursor, June to July 2025: the 20-dollar plan became a 20-dollar credit pool, followed by an apology and refunds (search-result only) [@techbriefly2025cursor; @leaddevaicoding]. Claude Code: weekly limits from 2025, and on 2026-08-29 a change the company itself described as a 17% reduction against that day's limits (search-result only) [@bleepingcomputeranthropic]. GitHub Copilot: usage-based "AI Credits" from 2026-06-01 (secondary, page read for this file) [@register2026github].
   - Plans on the vendors' pages, 2026-11-17 (primary): Copilot Pro 10 dollars a month with 15 dollars of credits, Pro+ 39 with 70, Max 100 with 200 [@github2026copilot]; Codex Go 8, Plus 20, Pro at 100, 200 or 500 [@openai2026codex]; Claude Pro 18 euros, Max 90, Ultra 180 [@anthropic2026claudesubscription]; Cursor Plus 8, Premium 30, Super 100, Ultra 200, with third-party model use billed separately [@cursor2026pricing].
   - Monthly AI cost per person, for a log-scale bar chart with the source type on each bar: Ramp median firm 12 to 15 dollars per employee, all AI vendors (secondary, no bib key yet); Anthropic's enterprise average for Claude Code 150 to 250 per developer (primary, vendor) [@anthropicclaudecode]; Ramp top 10% of firms 600 to 1,000; Uber's cap 1,500 per employee per tool (secondary, a policy, not a measurement); Gartner analyst statement for heavy users 2,000 to 5,000 (secondary, no method given); Ramp top 1% just under 10,000.
   - Plan against outcome, Menlo survey of nearly 400 US enterprise buyers, September 2026 (primary): mean spend 4.7 times the 2025 level against a planned 1.9 times; median 2.2 times against a planned 1.8 times [@menlo2026stateenterpriseai].
   - Developers' side, JetBrains 2026 raw data (primary, self-selected sample): 50% bought extra credits or quota last month for at least one tool (n = 5,264); 40% pay personally for at least one work tool; among those who do not use agents at work, 52% say "expensive" (n = 862); company "very" or "somewhat" concerned about spend 53% (n = 1,928); no cost controls at all 34% (n = 1,915) [@jetbrains2026rawdata].
   - Slide number, if any: 4.7 against 1.9.

3. Quotes.
   - "It now powers far more complex, agentic workflows that consume far more compute. This change is designed to deliver a more sustainable and reliable product experience by aligning pricing to actual usage and costs" [@register2026github]. Speaker: GitHub's April 2026 announcement, as quoted by The Register. Role and interest: the vendor explaining its own price change. Result: EXACT (Q4).
   - "This is a staggering shift from a 'predictable subscription' to a 'stressful meter-based' service that hinders my productivity rather than helping it" [@register2026github]. Speaker: an unnamed Copilot Pro+ subscriber on GitHub's user forum, as quoted by The Register. Role and interest: a paying customer, one voice, not a sample. Result: EXACT (Q5).
   - Backup, vendor's own figure: "Across enterprise deployments, the average cost is around $13 per developer per active day and $150-250 per developer per month, with costs remaining below $30 per active day for 90% of users." [@anthropicclaudecode]. Speaker: Anthropic's Claude Code documentation, no method or sample stated. Result: EXACT (Q6).

4. Example or case. Uber. TechCrunch, citing Bloomberg, reports "a monthly $1,500 cap per employee and per agentic coding tool, including Anthropic’s Claude Code or Cursor" (EXACT, Q7), after the company "had blown through its entire annual AI budget in a matter of four months" (EXACT, Q8). The same article says Uber had encouraged staff to use AI as much as possible and ranked usage on internal leader boards. This is secondary reporting and has no bib key yet.

5. Counter-evidence, in proportion. The median company is close to plan (2.2 against 1.8 times), and Menlo explains the mean this way: "a small group of power users accounts for a disproportionate share of AI spend and grew far faster than most enterprises" (EXACT, Q9). At the median firm AI still costs about as much as one enterprise seat. The vendor's average of 150 to 250 dollars a month is small next to a salary. In the same JetBrains sample 65% expect their company to spend more next year and 44% say the organisation is satisfied with the return (n about 1,900 to 1,980). Ramp's November reading shows token spend growth slowing to 1.5% across August and September. Gartner's forecast that coding-agent costs will overtake the average developer's salary by 2028 is an unscored prediction whose own release was not located.

6. Speaker-note material. The three repricing episodes are independent of each other, which is what makes the reading that flat plans were sold below cost to heavy users credible; the margin figures behind it (Cursor negative gross margin, about minus 23% to minus 30%) are leak-based and secondary or search-result only [@newcomercursor; @texxrcursor], so they stay backstage. Correction from later notes: the earlier file had no sourced cost per developer; the later one gives a distribution with a fat tail and no single value. Menlo's interest: venture investor, Anthropic shareholder. Ramp sees only its own customers' card and bill-pay data. The Gartner sentence about 2,000 to 5,000 dollars is The Register's reported speech of analyst Nitish Tyagi, not a direct quotation. Bain's line for the buyer's side: "Opex is migrating from headcount to token spend." (EXACT, Q10) [@bain2026technology].

7. Confidence and thesis. High that flat plans were withdrawn and that plans are now usage pools (primary pages). Medium for the size of the overrun (one investor's survey). Low for any single "cost per developer". Supports T14 (token cost as a business bottleneck) and qualifies T6.

8. Question for the audience. Who in your organisation owns the token budget: the developer, the team lead, or nobody?

9. Personal angle. T17 gives daily use across office, admin, teaching and research work. Plain sentence without a number: "The subscription used to feel like electricity at a fixed price; now there is a meter, and I look at it." Speaker to supply: the first time he hit a usage limit or bought extra credits, if that happened, and what he did next.

## I5.3 Physical limits arrive as a price and a waiting list, not as a wall

1. Title as a claim. Physical limits arrive as a price and a waiting list, not as a wall.

2. Number or data series.
   - Electricity, IEA (primary): data centres used about 415 TWh in 2024, about 1.5% of world electricity; demand grew 17% in 2025 against 3% for all electricity; Base Case about 945 TWh in 2030, just under 3%; 2035 scenarios 700 (Headwinds), 970 (High Efficiency), 1,200 (Base), above 1,700 TWh (Lift-Off) [@iea2025aichapter; @iea2025aiexecutive; @iea2026keyquestions].
   - Power equipment (search-result only, verify before any slide): GE Vernova gas-turbine backlog about 100 GW in Q1 2026 with delivery slots reported out to 2031; generator step-up transformer lead times above 160 weeks in Q1 2026 against an average of 143 weeks in 2024 [@nextwebpower; @woodmackenziesupply; @cryptobriefinggasturbine].
   - Memory, 2026 (secondary, page read for this file): IDC forecasts PCs minus 11.3% and smartphones minus 12.9% with devices about 14% dearer; Gartner PCs more than minus 10%, smartphones about minus 8%; Dell reports DRAM up nearly 5.5 times in six months and NAND nearly 4 times; HP says memory is 35% of the cost of materials of a PC, up from 15 to 18% a quarter earlier [@register2026memory; @techcrunch2026memory].
   - DRAM contract price change per quarter, TrendForce, different forecast vintages (search-result only): Q1 2026 plus 90 to 95%; Q2 plus 58 to 63%; Q3 plus 13 to 18% (server); Q4 plus 10 to 15% [@techzinememory; @tweaktowndramprices; @trendforce2026centre].
   - Stargate, Epoch AI, April 2026 (primary, page read for this file): about 0.3 GW operating, all at Abilene, against over 9 GW planned at seven sites (the site table sums to 9.3 GW plus one site below 0.3 GW); six of seven sites target Q4 2028 [@epochai2026where].
   - Scarcity inside a vendor's accounts (primary): Anthropic's Q3 2026 operating loss is attributed mainly to 5.0 billion dollars of compute bought under short-term commitments at a higher price than its long-term contracts [@anthropic2026pbcform].
   - Slide number, if any: 14% (the price rise for phones and PCs). The Stargate pair, 0.3 of more than 9 GW, is the picture.

3. Quotes.
   - "This is perhaps the biggest challenge the industry has faced since its inception" [@register2026memory]. Speaker: Nabilia Popal, senior research director for global consumer devices at IDC (name as spelt by The Register). Role and interest: market-research firm that sells forecasts to the device industry. Result: EXACT (Q11).
   - "a 3% share in 2030 means that data centre share in global electricity demand remains limited" [@iea2025aichapter]. Speaker: IEA report text. Role and interest: intergovernmental agency, generally conservative. Result: EXACT (Q12).
   - Backup: "$5.0 billion of compute under short-term commitments, which currently costs significantly more than comparable compute procured under our multi-year commitments" [@anthropic2026pbcform]. Speaker: Anthropic's S-1/A, the issuer under securities-law liability. Result: EXACT (Q13).

4. Example or case. The memory episode is the case where the AI build-out reached an ordinary desk: servers took the memory, and the next phone or laptop has less of it and costs more. For the Stargate picture, Epoch notes that builders put gas plants on site to avoid grid queues and use closed-loop cooling, and that "These decisions will likely save the project time but raise the cost of the facilities." (EXACT, Q14). The same page records that OpenAI dropped a planned expansion of the Abilene site to 2.1 GW.

5. Counter-evidence, in proportion. The IEA's own conclusion is that about 3% of world electricity in 2030 "remains limited". The IEA also reports that power use per AI task is declining rapidly. DRAM price increases slowed through 2026. A 13-fold yearly fall in the price of a fixed capability means the same workload needs far less hardware each year. Stargate's schedule for most sites is 2028, so a small operating share in 2026 is partly a matter of timing, and Oracle publicly disputes reports of trouble at Abilene (search-result only) [@tomshardwareoracle]. Which force dominates in 2027 and 2028 is not settled by the sources.

6. Speaker-note material. Corrections from later notes: the earlier file gave 485 TWh for 2025 and a US and China series (224 to 426 TWh, 117 to 277 TWh); the later file found that the IEA pages give no absolute 2025 figure and that the regional series is not on the pages read, so use 415 TWh (2024), plus 17% (2025) and 945 TWh (2030). Correction found while checking the Epoch page for this file: the earlier note said 9.6 GW planned and four of seven sites on on-site gas; the page says "over 9 GW" and "at least three of the seven sites". The note's statement that Epoch halved its own operating estimate from 0.6 to 0.3 GW was not visible in the scraped page and stays unconfirmed. Turbine, transformer, packaging and DRAM-contract figures all come from search summaries of trade press and market-research firms that sell forecasts. On parallelisation (named in T8) the notes found no published argument; that part would be the speaker's own reasoning.

7. Confidence and thesis. High for the IEA and Epoch figures (primary). Medium for the device forecasts (secondary, forecasts are revised). Low for equipment lead times (search-result only). Supports T8 in its moderate form and the physical-limits part of T14: limits reprice and delay, they do not stop.

8. Question for the audience. If compute is rationed by price and by queue, who gets served first, and where does a mid-sized company or a university stand in that queue?

9. Personal angle. Nothing in the brief ties the speaker's practice to energy or hardware. Plain sentence without a number: "I will never see a data centre, but I will see the limit, as a slower answer in the afternoon and a dearer laptop." Speaker to supply: a moment when a tool was rate-limited or unavailable at the time he needed it, or a recent hardware purchase where memory made the price.

## I5.4 The money question is about promises to pay, not about fraud

1. Title as a claim. The money question is about promises to pay, not about fraud.

2. Number or data series.
   - One issuer's commitments (primary): Anthropic reports aggregate committed spend of about 900.9 billion dollars, of which about 451.6 billion before the end of 2030 and about 70.7 billion within 12 months of 2026-09-30, against revenue of 33.2 billion for the first nine months of 2026. The commitments are about 27 times nine-month revenue (own arithmetic) [@anthropic2026pbcform].
   - IMF count of 14 public AI financing transactions, October 2026 (briefing transcript read locally, no bib key yet): 300 billion dollars of equity and 650 billion of debt in exchange for 1.4 trillion of future revenue commitments. Kristalina Georgieva, 2026-11-16, via CNBC (secondary, no bib key yet): circular financing a little over 1 trillion, two thirds of it debt.
   - Big-technology capital expenditure, mixed company sets, label each point: more than 400 billion dollars in 2025 for five firms and a further 75% expected in 2026 (IEA press release, primary) [@iea2026keyquestions]; 803 to 828 billion for five firms including SpaceX in calendar 2026, with Q2 at 188.5 billion and Q3 at about 227 billion (secondary) [@businessinsider2026amazon].
   - Required revenue according to an insider sceptic, David Cahn (primary): 200 billion dollars a year (September 2023), 600 billion (June 2024), 840 billion (June 2025), 1.5 trillion (July 2026) [@cahn2026ai5tquestion; @cahn2024ai600b]. Bain's version: an AI market approaching 6 trillion a year needed by 2031, with about 4.2 trillion of new revenue still to find [@bain2026technologyai].
   - The revenue side (primary): Anthropic quarterly revenue in million dollars, Q1 2025 376; Q2 787; Q3 1,382; Q4 2,040; Q1 2026 4,731; Q2 11,555; Q3 16,956. Operating result in 2026: Q1 minus 1,667; Q2 plus 974; Q3 minus 567 [@anthropic2026pbcform].
   - Slide number, if any: 900.9 billion dollars of commitments, shown beside 33.2 billion of nine-month revenue. Use at most this one pair.

3. Quotes.
   - "$300 billion in equity, $650 billion in debt, including in off‑balance sheet vehicles, in exchange for $1.4 trillion of future revenue commitments". Speaker: Jason Wu, Assistant Director, IMF, at the Global Financial Stability Report press briefing, October 2026. Role and interest: intergovernmental lender with a surveillance mandate, no commercial interest. Result: EXACT (Q15). The transcript has no bib key yet; the report itself is [@imf2026global].
   - "It is interesting, actually, that valuations are not stretched. P/E ratios actually have fallen because earnings keep surprising to the upside." Speaker: Athanasios Vamvakidis, Deputy Director, IMF, same briefing. Result: EXACT (Q16). He adds on whether it can continue: "Can this continue? It might. But it increasingly becomes more difficult." (EXACT, Q17).
   - Backup: "The increasing indebtedness of AI firms combined with opacity and, at times, ‘circular arrangements’ that can be associated with this financing, can complicate the assessment of risks and could amplify losses if expectations disappoint." [@bankofengland2026financialpolicy]. Speaker: Bank of England Financial Policy Committee, record of the 2026-09-25 meeting. Role and interest: central bank with a financial-stability mandate. Result: EXACT (Q18).

4. Example or case. One institution states both sides in one press briefing: the IMF counts the debt and the circular revenue promises, and its deputy director says that, unlike 2000, valuations are not stretched on earnings because earnings keep beating forecasts. A second, more personal case is David Cahn of Sequoia, an AI investor who has raised his own "revenue needed" figure every year since 2023 and writes in the same 2026 post: "When you see a company go $0 to $60B in ARR, we should all stand in awe of that performance." (EXACT, Q19) [@cahn2026ai5tquestion].

5. Counter-evidence, in proportion. Revenue is real and on an audited track: 33.2 billion dollars in nine months at one lab, with customer-serving compute at about 15% of revenue (own arithmetic from the filing). The net losses of tens of billions that made headlines are mostly non-cash fair-value changes caused by a rising valuation; the operating line is the one to show. The depreciation argument of the short-seller Michael Burry is unproven on current GPU rental-price data (secondary) [@247wallst2026burry]. The warning side is equally official: the IMF lists an AI correction first among three risks, and the Bank of England writes of AI firms' borrowing: "This pace of investment is unprecedented historically." (EXACT, Q20) [@bankofengland2026financial]. The two camps agree on the facts and differ on how long revenue can keep compounding.

6. Speaker-note material. Corrections from later notes: the earlier file quoted 518 billion dollars of planned compute spend from press reports of the confidential draft; the public filing gives 900.9 billion and supersedes it. The earlier Bank of England wording was about stretched equity valuations (October 2025, as recorded in the notes; no local copy) [@bankofengland2025financial]; by 2026 the language is about debt and circularity. Do not conflate two different figures of 1.4 trillion dollars: the IMF's count of revenue commitments across 14 deals, and OpenAI's reported infrastructure commitments (Reuters via an aggregator, search-result only) [@reuters2025streetinsider]. The GFSR sentence on AI: "Weaker or less durable investment and profitability in artificial intelligence could lead to a correction in AI-related asset prices" (EXACT, Q21) [@imf2026global]. Georgieva's line "It’s not a huge size, but it is a rapid increase" is EXACT (Q22) against the CNBC page, which is secondary and has no key yet. That CNBC page also carries the unverified Nvidia items listed under Open points; do not repeat them. Interests: Cahn and Sequoia hold AI investments; Bain sells advice; Burry holds a short position.

7. Confidence and thesis. High for the filing and the central-bank and IMF texts (primary). Medium for capex totals (mixed company sets, partly secondary). Tests T7 and T8 from the financing side and supports neither "bubble" nor "no bubble": the evidence shows capital intensity without precedent, financed partly by suppliers and by debt. Consequence for users, as an inference of the notes: if financing tightens, expect higher prices and tighter limits, not disappearance of the tools.

8. Question for the audience. If the suppliers' promises are not met in full, what changes first for you: the price, the limits, or the vendor?

9. Personal angle. The brief asks for no investment recommendations and has no personal financing story. Plain sentence without a number: "I do not know whether this is a bubble, and I notice that the people paid to know say both things in the same press conference." Speaker to supply: nothing required; this insight works without a personal story.
