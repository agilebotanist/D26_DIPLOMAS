The through-line of this theme: agentic AI made the production of software cheap, and it did not make anything else in the software business cheap. Every force in Porter's picture moved against the company that sells code or hours. What kept its value is what was never code: customers, data, the right to operate, and someone who answers for the result.

No management-journal analysis applying the five forces to software or IT services under agentic AI was found (see I7.6, item 6). The application below is the speaker's own synthesis and should be presented as such.

## Five-line summary

1. The one thing to remember: building software became cheap and selling it did not, so the value moved from the code to whatever cannot be regenerated overnight: customers, data, permission to operate, and accountability.
2. The best story: Miro. Founded in Perm in 2011, valued at 17.5 billion dollars in the 2021 round, sold on 26 October 2026 at an enterprise value of 1.355 billion while profitable and still growing. The honest ending is that nobody independent says AI did it.
3. The best picture: the five forces cross, drawn by hand, with one short phrase on each arm and the thickest arrow coming from the buyers (drawing instructions follow the summary).
4. The best question for discussion: if a competitor could regenerate your product's code in a week, what would your customers still be paying you for?
5. What the speaker should not claim: that agents have replaced a major SaaS product in production anywhere (no such case was found), that AI caused Miro's price (no independent source says so), that one-person unicorns exist (none was found), that seat-based pricing is collapsing (Bain found no vendor that left seats, and Atlassian reports seats growing), or that the five forces reading comes from a published study (it is his own).

## How to draw the five forces picture

Draw a cross. Rivalry sits in the box in the middle; four arrows point at it. One short phrase per force:

- Top, new entrants: "Easy to build, hard to sell."
- Bottom, substitutes: "Why buy it if we can build it?"
- Left, suppliers: "Two labs and one chip maker, and they sell apps too."
- Right, buyers: "Same work, a quarter less."
- Centre, rivalry: "Everyone can write everything."

Draw the arrow from the buyers thickest. That is a judgement, and the reasoning is in I7.3: it is the one force where money has visibly changed hands (price cuts asked and granted, outcome contracts signed, growth of the largest services firms down to 1 to 3%). New entrants moved most by count (apps, commits, startups) and least by effect, because attention did not grow. Substitutes moved as a threat that buyers use in negotiation more than as a fact. Suppliers were already strong and became more concentrated. An unreviewed preprint that reassesses the five forces for agentic AI in general reaches a similar ranking, with the largest effect "most acutely on buyer bargaining power and competitive rivalry" [@majumder2026porters] (abstract only read; not checked as a quote), which is support of low weight.

If there is room, draw a ring around the cross and write on it what the arrows do not reach: customers, data, permission, accountability. That ring is I7.6.

## I7.1 Building got cheap, selling did not

1. Title as claim. The number of things built exploded in 2026; the number of people who want them did not, so the barrier to entry moved from writing software to reaching customers.

2. Number or data series.
   - Apple App Store new app submissions, Sensor Tower data [@9to5mac2026appstore; @gigazine2026appstore; @star2026vibecoded] (all secondary; Sensor Tower sells app analytics): first quarter of 2026 235,800, up 84% year over year; first half of 2026 about 560,000, close to the whole of 2025 (557,000 to about 600,000 depending on the source); previous record 890,000 in 2016. Downloads over the same period: up 3% in 2025 and 2% in the first half of 2026 [@gigazine2026appstore].
   - GitHub Octoverse 2026 [@github2026octoverse] (primary; GitHub sells Copilot; year to 30 September; earlier years recalculated on a new definition that includes private activity, so not comparable with the 2025 report). Commits are 2.7 times the previous year (own arithmetic). Share of pull requests started with an agent: 17% in March 2026, more than 40% in September 2026. The 2025 report, on the old definition, gave "nearly 1 billion commits" and growth of 25.1% [@github2025octoverse] (primary).

Startup headcount peaked in 2021, a year before ChatGPT, so "AI makes startups smaller" is mixed with the end of cheap capital. ## I7.2 Agents can rebuild what has a specification and a test suite; nobody is known to run an agent-built replacement of a major SaaS product

1. Title as claim. Several well-known systems were rebuilt by agents for the price of a used car, a third of firms say they skipped a purchase because they could build, and still no documented case exists of a large company running an agent-built replacement of a major SaaS product in production at scale.

2. Number or data series.
   - McKinsey Global Survey on AI 2026 [@mckinsey2026stateofai] (primary, full report read in the notes; McKinsey sells AI transformation work): 32% of respondents say their organisation decided against buying at least one software product or feature because it could be built with coding agents; nearly half of "high performers" (92 respondents) against 31% of the rest. By industry: technology 41, healthcare 39, professional services 38, energy and materials 38, financial institutions 36, media and telecom 34, pharma 33, travel and logistics 31 (the list continues in Exhibit 4). The figure counts "at least one product or feature", which can be one internal tool; it does not measure displaced spending.
   - Rebuild cases, cost and time as stated by the builder. C compiler: just under 20,000 dollars in API fees, about two weeks, 16 parallel agents, about 100,000 lines of Rust [@carlini2026engineering] (primary, vendor showcase). Next.js-compatible framework: about 1,100 dollars in tokens, about one week, one engineer directing, about 94% of the API surface [@cloudflare2026vinext] (primary; Cloudflare competes with the framework's owner). Browser engine: about a week of agents, about 3 million lines including vendored code [@register2026cursor] (secondary). Character-detection library: an AI-assisted rewrite of a package with about 130 million monthly downloads, followed by a licence change from LGPL to MIT [@register2026chardet; @phoronix2026chardet] (secondary).
   - Amazon, Java upgrades [@jassy2024amazonq; @amazonwebservices2024awsdevops] (both primary; Amazon sells the tool): 50 developer-days per application down to "a few hours"; an estimated 4,500 developer-years saved; an estimated 260 million dollars a year in efficiency gains.
   - AWS Transform, one year on [@amazonwebservices2026transform] (primary, vendor marketing, self-reported, no audited customer figures): 4.5 billion lines of code processed; 250,000-line mainframe applications transformed and tested in six weeks; 810 developer-years saved "across thousands of customers".

5. Counter-evidence, in proportion. The rebuild cases are real, and the caveats are the same every time: an existing specification, a reference implementation or a large test suite; an expert directing the agents; quality below production grade; and no evidence yet of a year of maintenance. Nobody copied the installed base, the hosting business, the support organisation, the certification or the data.

## I7.3 Buyers moved most: every public productivity claim came back as a discount request

1. Title as claim. Clients of IT services firms, auditors and software vendors now ask for their share of the AI saving, and they are getting it before the saving is proven.

2. Number or data series.
   - IT services, Reuters, 21 August 2026 [@reuters2026starai] (secondary, wire report; re-read for this file): clients of Persistent Systems are "demanding the same work for 25% to 30% less" (the journalist's paraphrase of chief executive Sandeep Kalra). Growth of TCS, Infosys, Wipro and HCLTech in April to June 2026: 1 to 3%; Persistent Systems 16%. About 80% of TCS contracts in finance, HR and business services tie price to outcomes, double the share of late 2023.    - Expectation gap, BCG survey of more than 115 enterprise and more than 75 provider executives, February 2026 [@bcg2026billion] (primary; BCG sells to both sides): enterprises expect productivity gains of 30 to 40%; providers commit to 6 to 15%; nearly 60% of enterprises saw no measurable cost improvement yet.

## I7.6 Nobody defends the code any more; the argument is about who holds control

1. Title as claim. The analysts and investors who disagree about almost everything agree that code and user interface are no longer defensible, and they disagree on where control moves: to the system of record, to the agent layer above it, or to whoever sells the finished work.

2. Number or data series. This insight rests on positions, not measurements. The positions, each read at its primary page in the gaps file:
   - Stratechery [@thompson2026microsoft; @thompson2026appsagents]: customers want a supported, compliant, integrated product, not code; every vendor can now write unlimited software, so rivalry rises; later in the year, the agent that holds the user's context is the control point, and applications become suppliers to it.
   - a16z [@wang2025bigideas; @a16z2026systemintelligence]: the system of record loses primacy and becomes an input; control moves to the orchestration layer that accumulates workflows and context. In an earlier essay the same firm wrote that distribution is the default winner and incumbents have it [@a16z2024capitallabor].
