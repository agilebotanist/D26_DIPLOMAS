## Cognizant announces 4,000 job cuts tied to Project Leap, balancing AI‑driven automation with fresh hiring as demand slows across banking and technology sectors.

New Update

![Project Leap](https://img-cdn.publive.online/fit-in/1280x960/filters:format(webp)/dq/media/media_files/2026/05/01/project-leap-2026-05-01-16-56-30.png)

Project Leap

Cognizant Technology Solutions has announced plans to reduce its workforce by around 4,000 roles, or 1% of its global headcount, under a restructuring programme called Project Leap. The initiative is designed to accelerate adoption of artificial intelligence (AI), expand digital capabilities, and upskill employees, while streamlining operations to respond to weaker client demand.

The company expects restructuring costs of USD 230–320 million, including USD 200–270 million in severance and personnel expenses, but anticipates savings of up to USD 300 million this year.

## Demand slowdown in key markets

The job cuts come at a time when demand in banking and technology has softened. Clients are reducing discretionary spending, delaying projects, and focusing on cost optimisation. This slowdown has forced IT services firms, including Cognizant, to recalibrate cost structures and delivery models.

## Project Leap: A transformation programme

Project Leap is not just about workforce reduction. It is a broader transformation programme aimed at reshaping Cognizant’s operating model. The company is betting on AI‑ready services and digital transformation offerings to drive future growth.

## Financial and market context

Cognizant reported Q1 2026 revenue of USD 5.41 billion, up 5.8% year‑on‑year. Net profit stood at USD 662 million, slightly higher sequentially but lower compared to last year. Operating margin was 15.6%, down 40 basis points. Despite the layoffs, Cognizant’s headcount rose to 357,600 employees as of March 2026, a net addition of 6,000.

Some experts note that Cognizant’s move reflects a broader IT services trend. Firms are moving away from labour‑intensive delivery models toward AI‑augmented frameworks. Companies such as Oracle and Tata Consultancy Services have also announced layoffs recently, underscoring sector‑wide restructuring. Analysts highlight that cost discipline and AI investments are now inseparable, with firms reinvesting savings into consulting and industry‑specific solutions.

For Cognizant, Project Leap highlights how jobs are not disappearing outright but evolving rapidly.
