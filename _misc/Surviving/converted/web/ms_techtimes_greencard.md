# Microsoft's 60,000-Worker Buyout Offer Follows Freeze of Its Green Card Program

**URL:** https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329579/20261027/microsofts-60000-worker-buyout-offer-follows-freeze-its-green-card-program.htm
**Description:** Microsoft layoffs 2026: the company offered voluntary buyouts to 60,000 employees -- roughly 27 percent of its global workforce -- on October 26, just 18 days after the Department of Labor suspended
**Scraped:** 2026-11-17T14:57:40.859592+00:00

---

[Microsoft](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317458/20260531/microsoft-copilot-shifts-agent-governance-claude-checks-gpt-work-screen-agents-go-live.htm "Microsoft Copilot Shifts to Agent Governance: Claude Checks GPT Work, Screen Agents Go Live") launched the largest voluntary exit program in its 51-year history Sunday, offering buyout packages to approximately 60,000 employees — roughly one-quarter of its global workforce — just 18 days after the Department of Labor suspended the company from the federal green card sponsorship program that tens of thousands of its visa-holding workers depend on to build a permanent future in the United States. The company [confirmed the offer to TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/) on Monday. The collision of those two events transforms what Microsoft's Chief People Officer framed as an expanded choice into something considerably more fraught for a significant subset of the people receiving it.

The Voluntary Exit Offer, described in a Sunday memo from Amy Coleman, Executive Vice President and Chief People Officer, extends to employees at the vice president level and below across four countries: the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and India. Workers who accept the offer will receive financial compensation broadly similar to standard Microsoft severance, plus career planning resources. They have [until early December to decide](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-msft-voluntary-buyouts.html); those who opt in will exit at the end of the year.

What the memo does not address is the green card problem that the Department of Labor handed Microsoft workers on October 8, 2026.

### Green Card Complication — What H-1B Workers Must Weigh Before Signing

On October 8, 2026 — 18 days before Coleman's memo arrived in employees' inboxes — the Department of Labor announced it was [suspending Microsoft from the PERM program](https://wolfsdorf.com/dol-announces-perm-suspensions-for-microsoft-adobe-and-major-it-firms/), the formal labor certification process that serves as the first mandatory step for workers seeking employment-based green cards under the EB-2 and EB-3 preference categories. The suspension was made under 20 C.F.R. § 656.31(b), which authorizes the DOL to halt PERM processing for up to 180 days — a period extendable indefinitely while federal investigations or court proceedings remain unresolved.

For Microsoft workers on H-1B visas who have already submitted PERM applications, those applications are now paused. An employee who accepts the Voluntary Exit Offer and leaves Microsoft's sponsorship forfeits any pending PERM labor certification tied to that employment — and, depending on how long the suspension lasts, may not be able to transfer the process to a new employer without restarting at the beginning.

The scale of exposure is substantial. Microsoft received 6,258 H-1B visa approvals in fiscal year 2025, making it the third-largest H-1B sponsor among all US employers — and approximately 78% of those petitions were for existing employees rather than overseas hires, meaning a large share of Microsoft's H-1B workforce is actively embedded in long-term immigration plans. The PERM suspension cuts directly across those plans for workers who have filed or were preparing to file.

The stakes are sharpest for Indian-born employees, who are disproportionately represented in Microsoft's technical workforce and face a [green card backlog stretching decades](https://wolfsdorf.com/june-2026-visa-bulletin-sharp-retrogression-for-india-eb-1-and-eb-2-signals-mounting-pressure-on-employment-based-visa-numbers/) even after PERM approval clears — meaning any delay in the PERM process is not a minor inconvenience but a potentially career-defining setback.

Vice President JD Vance publicly characterized the DOL suspension as a response to Microsoft's alleged pattern of laying off American workers while filing H-1B applications to replace them with overseas hires. [Microsoft disputed the characterization](https://www.cfodive.com/news/microsoft-says-h1-b-visas-layoffs-unrelated-immigration/754255/), saying its H-1B applications are "in no way related to the recent job eliminations" and that it files visa petitions "only for eligible individuals" and pays H-1B workers comparably to colleagues in similar roles. The DOL investigation that prompted the suspension has not yet reached a public finding.

Immigration attorneys at Wolfsdorf Law noted in an October 8 advisory that [affected employees should seek](https://wolfsdorf.com/dol-announces-perm-suspensions-for-microsoft-adobe-and-major-it-firms/) "case-specific advice before making staffing decisions" given the uncertainty introduced by the suspension. The memo arriving 18 days later puts that advice in sharper relief: for workers with pending PERM applications, "should I accept this offer" is simultaneously a financial question and an immigration question, and Microsoft's offer materials address only the former.

### Who Is Eligible and What Is Being Offered

The program is open to employees at the vice president level and below across the United States, Canada, United Kingdom, and India. [Eligible workers — representing approximately 27%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-msft-voluntary-buyouts.html) of Microsoft's total workforce of roughly 223,000 — began receiving individualized emails Sunday with the specific terms of their offer.

Employees focused on data centers are [excluded from the program](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/microsoft-to-offer-buyouts-to-60-000-workers-in-latest-cost-cut), as are some staff in certain sales and marketing functions. Bloomberg reported that Xbox, Windows, Copilot, and Finance are among the participating units, while some engineering and marketing teams have opted out.

In her memo, Coleman framed the program as a deliberate evolution in Microsoft's approach to managing workforce change. "The Voluntary Exit Offer reflects an important part of how we're evolving our approach to change: creating greater flexibility and, where possible, more ways for employees to shape their next chapter," she [wrote in the official memo](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/26/introducing-a-voluntary-exit-offer/). "This offer gives eligible employees who may be considering a future outside the company a new choice to make that transition now with support."

Microsoft told GeekWire it anticipates [a significantly lower uptake rate](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/microsoft-makes-new-voluntary-exit-offer-to-more-than-a-quarter-of-its-workforce-including-some-execs/) than the 30%-plus participation seen in its April 2026 retirement program, noting that the earlier offer targeted longer-tenured employees already weighing retirement — a different population than the broader VP-and-below pool now receiving offers.

### A Year of Cascading Cuts

Monday's announcement is the latest in a sustained campaign by Microsoft to reshape its workforce around AI-driven productivity — one that has combined voluntary and mandatory reductions in a pattern that industry observers increasingly describe as a new model for large-scale tech restructuring.

The timeline illustrates a deliberate ratchet effect:

In April 2026, Microsoft launched its first-ever voluntary retirement program, targeting roughly 8,750 US employees at or below the senior director level whose age plus years of service totaled at least 70 under a "Rule of 70" formula. More than 30% of those eligible accepted — a participation rate Microsoft cited as evidence of demand for voluntary exit pathways, and a precedent that directly informed the October design. Microsoft confirmed those participation details to GeekWire.

One month later, in May 2026, the company announced mandatory layoffs of approximately 6,000 employees — about 3% of the workforce at the time — affecting teams across all levels and geographies. CEO Satya Nadella publicly attributed the cuts to organizational restructuring in response to AI transformation, not individual performance.

July 2026 brought a further 4,800 mandatory cuts, with Xbox gaming absorbing the heaviest share. Microsoft also parted ways with several gaming studios during this period, including Compulsion Games and Double Fine Productions. In September 2026, an additional 268 Xbox employees in Washington state were let go, extending what had become an ongoing contraction in the gaming division.

In aggregate, the voluntary and involuntary reductions have taken Microsoft's headcount from roughly 228,000 at the end of 2025 to approximately 223,000 at the end of June 2026 — a total reduction of more than 20,000 positions over 18 months before the October VEO opened.

### Is 'Voluntary' Really Voluntary?

The program's framing as a choice has drawn pointed skepticism from workforce experts who argue that a buyout offered against a backdrop of recurring mandatory layoffs is not a neutral option.

"A buyout in a climate of layoffs is not a choice," said Melonie Boone, business psychologist and CEO of Boone Management Group. "It is a calculated transfer of risk from the balance sheet to the employee."

Richard Demeny, founder and chief technology officer of Canary Wharfian, was more direct: "It's PR."

Career coach Lisa Dupras of Elev8 Coaching framed the question as one of comparative terms: workers who decline this offer should expect that future separations, whether voluntary or mandatory, may come with less favorable terms.

Olga Kokhan, CEO of Tinkogroup, described the mechanics in business terms: "Voluntary exits are a softer mechanism to achieve similar outcomes with less disruption to workforce morale."

The historical record from Microsoft's own 2026 restructuring adds weight to these assessments. Workers who declined the April 2026 voluntary retirement program were subsequently included in the cohort of 6,000 mandatory layoffs announced in May 2026 — a sequence that documents the concrete risk of waiting. The October offer arrives with that precedent already on the table.

### AI Imperative Behind the Numbers

The through-line connecting each round of cuts is Microsoft's extraordinary bet on artificial intelligence. The company has committed $80 billion to AI infrastructure build-out — data centers, specialized hardware, and cloud capacity — while internally restructuring operations to rely more heavily on AI-assisted work.

That investment is producing documented financial returns. In fiscal year 2026, Microsoft's [Azure cloud business surpassed $100 billion](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/microsoft-azure-q4-revenue-surges-41-tops-100-billion-annual-revenue) in annual revenue — a milestone that underscored the shift from a software licensing company to a cloud and AI platform. Q1 FY2027 results, which are [scheduled for release Wednesday](https://www.financecalendar.com/event/msft-earnings-october-2026/) on the company's earnings call, will be the first opportunity for analysts to see how the VEO's costs register on the balance sheet.

In remarks to investors, Nadella has described the workforce transition not as a cost-cutting exercise but as an irreversible shift in how work gets done — one he has compared to the arrival of email and spreadsheets in offices decades earlier. He has acknowledged publicly that the AI backlash is real, while predicting that higher wages and broader productivity gains will follow — a position he has used to argue for a global AI responsibility pact that would secure public trust in the technology. "We are moving aggressively to add capacity aligned to the demand signals we see," Nadella told investors, referring to data center expansion — a remark that landed the same week Coleman's memo announced the largest voluntary departure program in the company's history.

### New Playbook for Workforce Reduction

The Voluntary Exit Offer represents something new not only for Microsoft but potentially for the technology industry at large. Mandatory layoffs have long been the dominant tool for large-scale workforce reductions, carrying documented costs in morale, public relations, legal compliance, and severance obligations. Voluntary buyout programs allow companies to achieve headcount targets at a pace of their own choosing, with employees self-selecting out rather than being identified for termination — and without triggering the federal WARN Act's 60-day advance-notice requirements, which apply only to mandatory mass layoffs at facilities with 50 or more affected workers.

Coleman signaled in July that Microsoft was studying whether to make voluntary exit programs a more regular part of its workforce management, possibly on an annual or ongoing basis. The scale of Monday's announcement — extending the mechanism to a quarter of the global workforce and to vice-president-level employees, a seniority tier rarely included in broad buyout programs — suggests the study has yielded an affirmative answer.

"The Voluntary Exit Offer is new for us," Coleman wrote in her memo. "We'll learn from it."

If a significant share of the 60,000 eligible employees accept, the program would constitute the largest single workforce-reduction event in Microsoft's history, surpassing the 18,000 jobs cut following the 2014 Nokia acquisition and the 10,000 cuts announced in 2023 in response to macroeconomic conditions.

### What Comes Next

Microsoft has not announced mandatory layoffs alongside the voluntary program, and the company has explicitly framed the VEO as a complement to, rather than a precursor of, forced cuts. But analysts note that voluntary and mandatory reductions have followed each other closely throughout 2026, and Coleman's language about exploring voluntary exit as a recurring mechanism leaves open the question of what follows once the December window closes.

For the tens of thousands of employees now reviewing their offers, the decision involves weighing standard severance terms against the uncertainty of staying at a company that has reduced its workforce by thousands of roles in each of the past several quarters. For the subset on H-1B visas whose green card applications were paused by the October 8 DOL suspension, that calculation also involves weighing the immigration cost of leaving now against the risk that the suspension is not resolved before future cuts make the decision involuntary.

Microsoft's Q1 FY2027 earnings call is scheduled for Wednesday. It will be the first opportunity for the company to address analysts' questions about the program's projected financial cost and, potentially, the unresolved status of its federal green card sponsorship program.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who is eligible for the Microsoft Voluntary Exit Offer, and how does it work?

The offer extends to Microsoft employees at the vice president level and below in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and India — roughly 60,000 people, or 27% of the company's global workforce of approximately 223,000. Eligible employees received individualized emails Sunday with the specific financial terms of their offer. The package includes financial compensation broadly in line with standard Microsoft severance and career planning resources. Workers have until early December to decide whether to accept; those who opt in will depart at the end of 2026. Employees focused on data centers are excluded, as are some staff in certain sales and marketing functions. Full [eligibility terms for the VEO](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/) are available through TechCrunch's reporting on the offer.

### What does the DOL PERM suspension mean for Microsoft employees on H-1B visas?

On October 8, 2026, the Department of Labor suspended Microsoft from the PERM program — the federal labor certification process that is the mandatory first step for employment-based green cards (EB-2 and EB-3). Under 20 C.F.R. § 656.31(b), the suspension can last up to 180 days and can be extended while investigations remain unresolved. Workers with pending PERM applications are in a holding pattern: those applications are paused, and an employee who accepts the Voluntary Exit Offer and leaves Microsoft's sponsorship would forfeit those pending applications. For Indian-born employees in particular, who face multi-decade backlogs in the green card queue, any interruption to the PERM process carries significant long-term consequences. Immigration attorneys have advised that [H-1B workers seek legal advice](https://wolfsdorf.com/dol-announces-perm-suspensions-for-microsoft-adobe-and-major-it-firms/) before making any staffing decisions.

### Does the WARN Act require Microsoft to give workers advance notice of these departures?

No. The federal Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act requires employers with 100 or more employees to give 60 days advance notice before plant closings or mass layoffs — but that requirement applies only to involuntary separations. A voluntary program in which employees self-select to depart does not trigger the WARN Act, regardless of how many employees participate. However, legal observers note that Microsoft's 2026 pattern — alternating voluntary and mandatory reduction rounds — may be structurally designed in part to keep the mandatory headcount at any individual site below WARN thresholds. If the voluntary program is followed by mandatory layoffs, the mandatory component would be analyzed separately for WARN compliance.

### Will AI-related restructuring like this become a recurring practice at large tech companies?

Microsoft has signaled it may treat voluntary exit programs as an ongoing workforce management tool rather than a one-time measure. CEO Satya Nadella has argued publicly that AI represents an irreversible shift in how work gets done — comparable to the arrival of email and spreadsheets — and that future hiring will be calibrated to generate more output from smaller teams. Workforce experts including Melonie Boone, CEO of Boone Management Group, have characterized large-scale voluntary buyouts in a climate of recurring layoffs as a structural feature of AI-driven workforce rebalancing rather than an isolated event. Microsoft's October program — extending voluntary exit terms to a quarter of the global workforce — represents the most expansive deployment of this model yet by a major technology employer, and its uptake rate and cost profile will be closely watched by peer companies.
