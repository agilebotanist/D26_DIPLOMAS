**Yegor Denisov-Blanch**, a Research Scientist at the **Stanford AI Lab**, led a large-scale study of nearly **100,000 developers** across 600+ companies to measure the real-world impact of AI on software engineering productivity. The research reveals that while AI provides an average productivity boost of **15–20%**, its effectiveness is highly dependent on **task complexity** and **project maturity** (Greenfield vs. Brownfield).

The study highlights a significant disparity in gains based on the codebase environment:

- **Greenfield Projects (New Code):** AI yields the highest productivity gains, particularly for **low-complexity tasks** (30–40%) and **high-complexity tasks** (10–15%).
- **Brownfield Projects (Existing Code):** Gains are more modest, with **low-complexity tasks** seeing 15–20% improvement and **high-complexity tasks** showing only **0–10%** gains, sometimes even reducing productivity due to increased rework and debugging.

Furthermore, the study notes that **popular programming languages** (e.g., Python, Java) see more significant benefits than niche languages, and that AI-generated code often introduces **rework** that offsets initial speed increases.
