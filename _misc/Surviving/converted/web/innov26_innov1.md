Gartner on Thursday forecast global spending on artificial intelligence (AI)-optimized servers to reach $957.2 billion in 2027, an increase of around 81% from $527.6 billion in 2026.

The research firm expects total worldwide AI spending to rise 47.5% to $4.05 trillion next year, with infrastructure accounting for approximately 59%, or $2.4 trillion.

The forecast comes amid renewed turbulence in AI-linked stocks, as uncertainty over Nvidia (NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang's future at the firm rattled investors following reports of two extramarital affairs, including one involving a woman suspected of ties to Chinese intelligence.

At the time of this writing, Nasdaq-100 futures were down 0.7%, with a selloff across semiconductor and AI infrastructure stocks.

## Why AI Server Spending Is Rising

Gartner attributed the projected spending growth to faster-than-anticipated expansion of AI inference workloads, which involve running trained AI models, alongside increasingly compute-intensive training requirements.

John-David Lovelock, Distinguished VP Analyst and Chief of Research at Gartner, said both trends continue to increase spending on AI infrastructure globally.

The firm also cited continued infrastructure expansion by hyperscalers and neocloud providers, alongside "memflation," its term for memory-driven increases in AI server prices.

Gartner said in April that meaningful relief from elevated memory prices was unlikely before late 2027. Lovelock separately told CIO Dive in October that memflation could persist into 2030.

The spending outlook is relevant to companies supplying AI servers and computing hardware, including Dell Technologies (DELL), Super Micro Computer (SMCI) and Nvidia (NVDA).

## AI Agents, Cybersecurity And Software Spending To Rise

Beyond infrastructure, Gartner expects spending on AI agents and assistants to more than double from $30 billion in 2026 to $67 billion in 2027, making it the fastest-growing category in its forecast.

AI cybersecurity spending is projected to increase from $51 billion in 2026 to $86 billion in 2027, representing approximately 69% growth, according to Gartner. The forecast puts cybersecurity companies such as CrowdStrike (CRWD), Palo Alto Networks (PANW), Zscaler (ZS) and Okta (OKTA) in focus.
