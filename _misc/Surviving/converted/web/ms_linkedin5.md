## LinkedIn axes hundreds as Bellevue office braces for cuts

LinkedIn is laying off about 850 employees globally, including workers in Bellevue, as the Microsoft-owned platform restructures despite posting a 12% revenue increase.

LinkedIn's Bellevue office at Lincoln Square North faces an uncertain outlook after the Microsoft-owned platform announced cuts affecting roughly 5 percent of its global workforce Wednesday, May 14, 2026 (Photo by Justin Sullivan/Getty Images)

(Photo by Justin Sullivan/Getty Images)

LinkedIn, the Microsoft-owned professional social-networking platform, notified hundreds of employees this week that they were out of a job, adding more bad news onto a regional tech labor market that has been shedding positions for well over a year.

The layoffs, first reported by *[Bloomberg](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-13/microsoft-s-linkedin-is-cutting-jobs-in-latest-industry-cull)*, are expected to hit roughly 5 percent of LinkedIn’s global workforce of more than 17,000. Engineering, product, marketing, and the company’s global business organization are all taking cuts. Several Seattle-area employees confirmed their departures in posts on the platform itself; [MyNorthwest reported](https://mynorthwest.com/local/microsoft-linkedin-layoffs/4238375) that the Washington Employment Security Department has not yet filed a WARN notice specifying how many Puget Sound-area workers were affected.

LinkedIn has maintained a Bellevue office on two floors at Lincoln Square North, a space that can hold at least 120 employees, since Microsoft acquired the platform in 2016. The company kept those floors even after Microsoft’s retreat from downtown Bellevue during the pandemic.

## New CEO, first major test

The cuts come just weeks into the tenure of a new chief executive.

LinkedIn promoted Chief Operating Officer Daniel Shapero to CEO less than a month ago. His predecessor, Ryan Roslansky, moved to Microsoft as an executive vice president overseeing LinkedIn and Microsoft Office. Shapero sent an all-hands memo to staff at 7 a.m. Wednesday morning.

“Today I’m sharing the difficult decision that I, along with our leadership team, have made to reduce roles across GBO, Marketing, Engineering and Product,” he wrote, according to a copy obtained by *Business Insider*. The company will also pull back spending on marketing campaigns, external vendors, customer events, and office space it no longer fills.

LinkedIn’s revenue grew 12 percent year-over-year in the most recent quarter, carried by recruiting tools and subscription services. Sources told Reuters the cuts are not about artificial intelligence taking jobs; the rationale is reorganizing teams and redirecting capital toward higher-priority areas.

### Washington’s tech sector takes another blow

[Washington’s tech sector has been absorbing wave after wave of job losses](https://seattlered.com/washington-state/wa-layoffs-surge-ai/4116615), and LinkedIn extends the streak.

Microsoft cut approximately 15,000 jobs globally in 2025, more than 3,200 of them in Washington state at its Redmond and Bellevue campuses. Now the company is [offering a one-time voluntary retirement buyout to roughly 8,750 U.S. employees](https://seattlered.com/economy/microsoft-income-tax-buyout-offer/4118203), with a decision deadline of June 8; those who accept will leave July 1, the start of Microsoft’s 2027 fiscal year.

The tech layoff environment is not improving in the region. Layoffs.fyi tracked more than 103,000 tech job eliminations in 2026 heading into this week, fast approaching the 124,000 total it counted for all of last year. [Seattle already leads the world in AI-linked tech job losses](https://seattlered.com/economy/seattle-ai-job-losses/4117132), with more than 16,590 employees affected by decisions made at companies headquartered in the region.
