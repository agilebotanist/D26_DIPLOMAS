# Microsoft cloud chief Scott Guthrie reportedly asks leaders to find 20% cost cuts

**Description:** Microsoft cloud president Scott Guthrie reportedly asked leaders to find 20% cost cuts as the company plans about $190 billion in AI capex.

The push for savings arrives as Microsoft plans roughly $190 billion in capital spending tied heavily to AI and data centers

[Microsoft](https://cryptobriefing.com/markets/microsoft/) is spending money like a company building the future. It is also asking its leaders to find savings like a company bracing for winter.

Scott Guthrie, the president of Microsoft’s cloud business, has asked leaders to identify cost cuts of 20%, according to reporting from The Information that other outlets later picked up. The request, dated October 13, 2026, lands in the middle of one of the most expensive infrastructure buildouts in corporate history.

## What Guthrie is asking for

The directive asks leaders to find strategic reductions of 20%. It reflects mounting pressure on enterprise spending, where efficiency has become the defining word of the year.

Back in March 2026, the company put a hiring freeze in place across its cloud unit and its North American sales groups. The stated goal was cost reduction.

Then came July. Microsoft announced job cuts of approximately 4,800 roles, or about 2.1% of its workforce.

Most of those positions came from the Xbox division. That unit is set to shrink its headcount by around 20%, which works out to roughly 3,200 jobs.

## The other side of the ledger

The company planned approximately $190 billion in capital spending for 2026. Most of that is pointed at AI and the data centers needed to run it.

In the third quarter of 2026 alone, Microsoft reported over $130 billion in new data center lease commitments. That figure covers new leases signed during the period.

The company is also pressing ahead with its own silicon. Plans call for scaling its Maia 200 AI chips to multi-gigawatt deployments, with that ramp set to begin in 2027.

Leadership is shifting as well. Around October 12 and 13, 2026, Microsoft announced changes to its Azure leadership, with Arun Ulag taking on an expanded role.

## What this means

For employees, the picture is more uncomfortable. A hiring freeze in March, job cuts in July and a 20% savings target in October form a clear trajectory, even if the cloud directive does not specify headcount reductions.

Cost cuts can come from many places: vendor contracts, travel, marketing, internal projects. How leaders choose to hit that 20% will determine whether this round stays in spreadsheets or reaches people.

A few things are worth tracking from here. First is whether the 20% target translates into further workforce changes in the cloud organization.

Second is how the Azure leadership reshuffle, including Arun Ulag’s expanded role, shapes priorities inside the cloud business.

Third is the trajectory of those data center commitments. Over $130 billion in new leases in a single quarter is a long-term bet, and any change in pace would say a lot about Microsoft’s confidence in AI demand.

Finally, there is the Maia 200 timeline. The 2027 start for multi-gigawatt deployments is a marker of whether Microsoft’s in-house chip strategy is on track, and whether owning more of its hardware can eventually help bring its own costs down.
