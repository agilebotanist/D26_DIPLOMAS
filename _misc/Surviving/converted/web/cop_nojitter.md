# Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption jumps 50% over prior quarter

**URL:** https://www.nojitter.com/digital-workplace/microsoft-365-copilot-adoption-jumps-50-percent-over-prior-quarter
**Description:** Paid Copilot now has 30 million seats, Microsoft said, doubling its adoption as a percent of paid M365 Commercial seats from January.

Paid Copilot now has 30 million seats, Microsoft said, doubling its adoption as a percent of paid M365 Commercial seats from January.

No Jitter Brief:

- Microsoft 365 Copilot reached 30 million paid seats, the company reported during its [fiscal Q4 2026 earnings call](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4927337-microsoft-corporation-msft-q4-2026-earnings-call-transcript) on Wednesday. This is a 50% quarter-over-quarter increase from the [20 million paid Copilot seats](/ai-automation/microsoft-365-copilot-hits-20-million-paid-seats) reported in fiscal Q3 2026.
- “Paid M365 Commercial seats grew 6% year-over-year,” said Satya Nadella, Microsoft’s chairman and CEO, during Wednesday’s earnings call. Microsoft did not report the number of seats in [fiscal Q4 2025](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4806519-microsoft-corporation-msft-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript), but did report that paid M365 commercial seats were [over 430 million](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4780224-microsoft-corporation-msft-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript) during its fiscal Q3 2025 earnings call. If Microsoft had 430 million paid M365 Commercial seats at the end of March 2025, and if paid seats grew 6% year over year for the fiscal quarter ended June 30, 2026, then paid M365 commercial seats could approximate 455.8 million.
- Microsoft reported more than [450 million paid M365 Commercial seats](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4863620-microsoft-corporation-msft-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript) in its [fiscal Q2 2026 earnings call](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4863620-microsoft-corporation-msft-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript) in January. That puts paid Copilot seats at approximately 6.6% to 6.7% of paid Commercial seats, using either the 450 million or 455.8 million estimates. In its Q2 2026, Microsoft’s paid Copilot seats approximated [3.3% of paid M365 Commercial seats](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/02/02/microsoft-reveals-just-33-of-copilot-chat-users-pay-for-it/4288251) — a number that was [much discussed](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/02/15/microsofts-small-number-paying-copilot-customers/) in [early 2026](/ai-automation/4-obstacles-impede-paid-microsoft-365-adoption).

No Jitter Insight:

Microsoft is sticking to the message that Copilot adoption and usage are a growing part of its profits. The company said the number of customers with more than 50,000 Copilot seats increased more than sevenfold year over year, while the number of enterprise customers deploying Copilot to the majority of their information workers grew nearly 75% quarter over quarter, with Nadella calling that stat “a signal of how central Copilot has become to \[their customers’\] operations.”

Microsoft cited several customers during the call to underscore this message. NHS England rolled out Copilot to 505,000 clinicians and staff “after a trial showed it saved employees an average of 43 minutes per day,” Nadella said, also citing KPMG’s rollout to its 276,000-strong workforce and HSBC committing to 200,000 seats.

Copilot conversations per user have “nearly doubled year over year. Average weekly engagement is on par with Outlook and Teams,” Nadella said. “The usage intensity itself has gone up significantly. I mean we are talking about usage intensity that’s sort of at the same level of what is an everyday communication tool like Outlook or Teams.”
