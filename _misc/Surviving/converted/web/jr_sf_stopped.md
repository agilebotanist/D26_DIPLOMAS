# Big Tech Quietly Stopped Hiring Junior Coders, and the Data Proves It

**URL:** https://startupfortune.com/big-tech-quietly-stopped-hiring-junior-coders-and-the-data-proves-it/
**Description:** SignalFire data shows entry-level tech hiring down 65-75% since 2019, as AI tools absorb junior coding work once done by new grads entering the field.
**Scraped:** 2026-11-17T15:01:41.347118+00:00

---

*Companies aren't firing junior developers over AI. They're doing something quieter: hiring far fewer of them, while keeping demand for experienced engineers alive.*

No one at Alphabet or Meta announced a mass firing of junior engineers this year. There was no press conference, no severance headline, no viral layoff spreadsheet. Something quieter happened instead: the entry-level rung of the career ladder stopped being built.

According to SignalFire's 2026 State of Tech Talent report, new-grad and entry-level hiring has fallen about 65% at the 12 Tech Majors compared with 2019. The group includes Alphabet, Meta, Apple, Amazon, Microsoft, Netflix, Nvidia, Tesla, Uber, Airbnb, Block and Stripe. At early-stage startups, SignalFire put the drop at roughly 76%.

Nobody needed to fire anyone. The req just never got posted.

Here's the twist buried inside the same report. Total hiring at those big technology companies is down 25% from the 2019 baseline, but engineering hiring is down only 11%. Early-stage startups hired 7% more engineers in 2025 than they did in 2019. AI isn't wiping out the demand for people who can write code. It's cutting the demand for people who still need months of hands-on training before they can carry production work.

That distinction matters, because the story isn't "robots took the jobs." It's closer to this: companies kept the senior engineer and dropped the apprenticeship.

Alicia Sasser Modestino, an economist at Northeastern University, studied the shift with Samuel Westby and Peiran Cheng in a June 2026 IZA discussion paper on entry-level software work. Using Lightcast online vacancy data, they found a 14% to 15% relative decline in junior software developer postings compared with senior ones after ChatGPT's public release. The main change wasn't that companies invented a new set of job titles. They raised the experience bar inside roles that used to welcome new grads.

Think about what junior developers have traditionally done all day: write boilerplate, generate test scripts, chase small bugs, clean up pull requests before a senior engineer even looks at them. Tools such as GitHub Copilot, Cursor and Claude Code now cover pieces of that work before a human starts the next pass. They still need review. Of course they do. But the cheapest training tasks in the codebase are exactly the ones companies are now most tempted to hand to software.

That is bad news for anyone trying to get a first job.

Stanford Digital Economy Lab researchers Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar and Ruyu Chen found the same pressure from another angle. In an August 2026 update to their "Canaries in the Coal Mine" work, they reported that employment among workers ages 22 to 25 in highly AI-exposed occupations stood about 19% below where it would be if it had kept pace with similarly aged workers in less-exposed roles. The shortfall was 15% in the July 2025 data vintage. By June 2026, it had widened.

Crucially, the Stanford team found no comparable gap among experienced workers. This isn't senior engineers getting shown the door. It's twenty-two year olds who can't get in it.

## The labor market isn't helping

The broader jobs picture makes the squeeze harder to dismiss. The US economy added 162,000 jobs in August 2026 and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. That sounds strong until you look at where the losses landed. The information sector shed 23,000 jobs in the same month, including losses in computing infrastructure providers, data processing, web hosting, publishing, broadcasting and content providers.

Those categories aren't the whole software labor market, and you shouldn't pretend they are. Still, they sit close enough to the work being changed by automation that the direction is hard to ignore. Hiring is not collapsing everywhere. It is getting older and more tilted toward people who already know what they're doing.

## Who gets the junior job now

Here's the part that should worry any twenty-two year old with a computer science degree: the remaining entry-level seat is no longer guaranteed to be entry-level in practice. SignalFire's report points to a large pool of experienced candidates competing for fewer openings, while companies prioritize senior individual contributors who can use AI tools to cover more ground with less support.

That's a bad trade for the actual junior. Frankly, it may be a bad trade for the company too.

Every senior engineer alive today learned by doing the grunt work that AI tools now absorb before a human ever sees it. Chasing a memory leak that only shows up in production, fixing a null-pointer exception at 2am, writing a test suite nobody will read closely, arguing with a code reviewer about naming conventions. That's not busywork. It's how judgment gets built.

If companies keep skipping that stage for an entire generation, the bill comes due later. In five or ten years, there won't be enough thirty-five year old engineers with the instincts that come from years of shipping, breaking things and cleaning up the mess. A slimmer team looks efficient on a spreadsheet in 2026. It looks less clever when nobody has trained the next tech lead.

SignalFire names this as the real risk: not that AI eliminates engineering, but that the industry stops investing in the people who would have become its next senior engineers. Founders rebuilding teams around fewer, AI-augmented seniors are solving for today's headcount cost. The harder question is who trains the person who would have been ready in 2031.

For now, the numbers are the story. Down about 65% at the Tech Majors. Down about 76% at early-stage startups. A 19% employment gap for young workers in highly AI-exposed occupations. Nobody got fired. The door just quietly stopped opening.
