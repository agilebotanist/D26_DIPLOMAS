# IT workforce diverges in Q2: TCS, HCLTech, Tech M, Infosys add employees as Wipro shrinks

**URL:** https://hr.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/industry/it-workforce-diverges-in-q2-tcs-hcltech-tech-m-infosys-add-employees-as-wipro-shrinks/135143853
**Description:** Discover the latest IT workforce trends for Q2 FY27 as TCS and HCLTech report employee growth, while Wipro faces reductions. Explore insights on attrition rates, fresher hiring, and the shifting demand for specialized skills in the IT industry.

Experts further mentioned IT staffing demand is increasingly shifting towards experienced professionals with specialised skills, particularly in artificial intelligence (AI), data engineering, cloud modernisation and cybersecurity, as companies prioritise immediate productivity and business outcomes over traditional bulk hiring.

India’s leading IT services companies reported mixed workforce trends in the second quarter of FY27, with differences emerging in employee retention, headcount growth and fresher hiring.Tata Consultancy Services (TCS), HCLTech, Wipro, Infosys and Tech Mahindra reported changes in their workforce numbers and attrition rates for the July to September quarter, offering a glimpse into how technology companies are balancing talent requirements with business demand and productivity priorities.

Advt

While TCS and Tech Mahindra reported a change in attrition, HCLTech significantly increased its fresher intake. Wipro, in contrast, recorded a decline in its employee headcount during the quarter.Suchita Dutta, Executive Director, ISF, said, “The divergence across India's top IT firms is itself a growth signal for staffing. In Q2 FY27, some majors posted their strongest net additions in 16 quarters while others saw workforces contract by several thousand - a recalibration, not a retreat. It is channelling demand into flexible and contract staffing: subcontractor intensity at leading firms is climbing toward 7 percent of revenue, and IT-services active openings hit an 18-month high of 57,000 in September 2026, with total technology demand near 117,000 roles.”
**Infosys adds 4,480 employees; attrition declines**

Infosys added 4,480 employees during Q2 FY27, taking its total workforce to 3,32,542 at the end of September, compared with 3,28,062 at the end of June. Its voluntary attrition rate in IT services declined to 12.7 percent from 13 percent in the preceding quarter. The company plans to recruit 20,000 fresh graduates in FY27 and roll out salary increases in phases in October 2026 and January 2027, with most employees expected to receive hikes in October and senior employees in January.

**TCS adds employees; HCLTech steps up fresher hiring**

TCS added 4,258 employees on a net basis during Q2 FY27, taking its total workforce to 5,98,056 employees as of September 30, 2026. This marked the third consecutive quarter of net headcount addition following the company's workforce restructuring in FY26.

Advt

Its last twelve month attrition rate declined to 13.3 percent from 13.6 percent in the preceding quarter.The company also reported an increase in employee learning, with learning hours rising 17 percent sequentially to 17.1 million during the quarter.

TCS had rolled out 25,000 offers to freshers at the beginning of FY27 and onboarded around 14,000 by the end of Q1. The company’s workforce disclosures indicate that hiring and employee development remain important parts of its talent strategy.

HCLTech recorded one of the strongest fresher hiring numbers among the four companies, onboarding 6,136 freshers in Q2 FY27, compared with 1,056 in the preceding quarter.

The company added 6,450 employees on a net basis during the September quarter, taking its total headcount to 2,30,339 employees. However, its voluntary attrition rate edged up to 12.8 percent from 12.7 percent in the previous quarter and 12.6 percent a year earlier.

The company's fresher intake during Q2 was also higher than the 5,196 freshers onboarded in the corresponding quarter of the previous financial year.

**Wipro's workforce shrinks by 6,716**

Wipro reported a net reduction of 6,716 employees during Q2 FY27, taking its workforce to 2,36,328 employees at the end of September, compared with 2,43,044 employees at the end of June.

Its voluntary attrition rate eased marginally to 13.8 percent from 13.9 percent in the previous quarter.

The decline in headcount, alongside the marginal improvement in attrition, presents a contrasting workforce picture compared with TCS and HCLTech, both of which added employees during the quarter.

However, the reduction in headcount should not be interpreted as a direct measure of voluntary exits or layoffs. Wipro's reported net workforce movement does not, by itself, establish how many employees left voluntarily, how many were let go or how many new employees were hired.

The company has not disclosed a detailed breakdown of fresher hiring in the cited Q2 workforce disclosures.

**Tech Mahindra reports lower attrition and higher headcount**

Tech Mahindra added 5,446 employees sequentially during Q2 FY27, taking its total workforce to 1,52,206 employees.

Its last-twelve-month IT attrition rate declined marginally to 11.7 percent from 11.8 percent in the previous quarter.

The increase in headcount follows a period in which the company focused on improving utilisation and margins. Its latest results also pointed to continued investment in artificial intelligence to improve productivity, drive innovation and build capabilities for future growth.

Its disclosed results did not specify a comparable fresher-hiring figure for the quarter.

**Shift in demand for talent**

Experts further mentioned [IT staffing demand](/tag/it+staffing+demand) is increasingly shifting towards experienced professionals with specialised skills, particularly in artificial intelligence (AI), data engineering, cloud modernisation and cybersecurity, as companies prioritise immediate productivity and business outcomes over traditional bulk hiring.

Sunil Nehra, CEO – IT Staffing, FirstMeridian, said, “Demand is shifting towards professionals who can apply their expertise to live business requirements and deliver results quickly. Data engineering, cloud modernisation, cybersecurity, enterprise platforms and AI implementation are key areas of demand, particularly as businesses upgrade existing systems and strengthen digital capabilities.”

Additionally, professionals with 4 to 10 years of experience remain the core of IT hiring, while AI hiring pressure is particularly high in the 3 to 5 year experience band, which accounts for 49.1 percent of active AI hiring demand. This reflects the growing need for professionals who can combine technical expertise with business understanding and deliver solutions with limited ramp-up time as per Kapil Joshi, CEO of IT Staffing, Quess Corp.

At last, Milind Shah, MD, Randstad Digital – India, added that contract staffing is rising as firms seek flexibility amid AI-driven efficiency and project-based demand. Overall, the staffing market is shifting towards agile, outcome-oriented models over traditional bulk hiring.”

He further said that scarcity is visible in compensation, with niche skills commanding a significant premium depending on expertise, complexity and business impact. Over the next two to three quarters, employers that pair upskilling with specialist hiring through flexible models will be best placed to move AI from pilot to production.

For the IT sector, the next quarters will show whether workforce expansion and fresher onboarding continue as companies pursue growth in AI led services while managing productivity, margins and employee retention.
