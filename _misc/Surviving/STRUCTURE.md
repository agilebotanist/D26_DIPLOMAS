# Talk structure: draft 2

Status: draft 2, 2026-11-18, rebuilt from the ground after the speaker judged draft 1 generic and not provocative. Draft 1 is kept in `~/tmp/_code/_DIPLOMAS/D26_DIPLOMAS/_misc/Surviving/backup_2026-11-18/`.

Topic: surviving in the agent era, as an IT specialist and as a business. Business and industry come first, because they explain to the specialist where the money is moving, and the jobs follow the money (speaker's decision, 2026-11-18).

## What changed from draft 1

- The talk is about the audience surviving, not about the speaker's corrected beliefs.
- Provocation comes from the speaker's own bets, stated in his name. Each bet rests on one hard fact on the slide, and the close says how he would know he is wrong. Research decides what is a fact; the speaker decides what to bet.
- Hedging moves into the speaker notes. A slide states one thing.
- The two frames stay, without lecture slides. Five forces is the hidden skeleton of Part 1 (buyers, suppliers, entrants, substitutes, one slide each). The S-curve appears as "the ruler ran out", in Part 2.

## The law of the talk

What became free: writing code, finding flaws, building an app, a first draft.
What did not: checking, deciding, being trusted, answering for the result, the customer's attention.
Survival means owning the second list. Part 1 shows a business paying for it; Part 2 shows a specialist becoming it.

4. Four months behind on a model nobody can take away, or at the frontier on one that can disappear tomorrow?
