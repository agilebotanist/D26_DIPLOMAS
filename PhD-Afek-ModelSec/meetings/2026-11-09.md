## What was presented

TTool-AI (an LLM layer over the SysML-Sec method) automates requirement extraction with security tags, SysML diagram generation, attack-graph generation (CAPEC only) and security countermeasures. Validation in the paper is a timing and correctness comparison against master students. SysML-Sec itself covers requirements, attack graphs, HW/SW partitioning, UPPAAL (safety) and ProVerif (security) verification, and code generation via SoCLib. Model mutation goes through AMULET. Afek also showed the MDE-assisted SDD vs AI-assisted MDE comparison table and the INCOSE MBSE copilot roadmap (levels of automation; level 3 is the target for agentic security).

## Points raised

1. The links between the colours in the SysML-Sec figure (requirements to attack graphs, countermeasures to design, verification) are the interesting part and are opaque. How countermeasures are chosen (which knowledge base), how they are placed in the architecture, and how they are verified against an attack scenario are all unknown. The figure comes from Letitia Li's 2018 thesis, so it is unclear which links are from 2018 and which are new in the AI version.
2. Known limits: LLM output is not reproducible (component names change between runs, which breaks traceability across diagrams); no dynamic or behavioural aspect; attack scenarios are defined manually; only CAPEC is used (MITRE ATT&CK suggested as an extension); explainability of a countermeasure is weak, yet audits against a standard require evidence.
3. SDD and SysML-Sec are not two alternatives of the same kind. SDD is an approach, SysML a language, and SysML-Sec requirements are already spec-driven. Compare them in a table of strengths and limits to find the gap. Andrey (as I read the recording): XML-style SysML is heavy for LLM context, so methods transfer but the format may not; TTool-AI handles context by slicing the prompt per stage.
4. Contribution risk: a single-element contribution can be overtaken within months. Prefer small, verifiable contributions. Possible gap categories: better validation by replaying an attack against the countermeasure, better countermeasure mapping, better placement in the architecture, dynamic aspects, conformity with a standard.
5. Validation idea: replay a concrete attack (for example weak authentication) against the generated countermeasure instead of comparing speed with students. Andrey (as I read the recording): look for a corpus of SDD-built projects and run security tools on them to motivate the problem; LLM-as-judge agents can help with V&V.
6. Domain: embedded systems alone leaves room but the supervision team lacks skills for pure systems work. IoT (light, mostly transport and data analysis) and ICS (existing norms and knowledge bases) were both discussed. Not decided; depends on the contribution.
7. One supervisor recommended a recent ICS risk-analysis thesis (defended 2025, industrial partner) and a thesis that extends beyond CAPEC to other knowledge bases; both are on the Drive. Limitations and perspectives sections are the place to look for gaps.
8. Access to IEEE and Springer papers: use the university library access through the library contact; Sci-Hub is a fallback (search by DOI for recent papers). A colleague offered to send PDFs on request.
